# Comic style

## What it does

The game's art style (always on: `Game.comic_style` stays true as the flag the art code reads), ported from the Blender kit in
`vendor_src/shaman_redesign/comic_poc`: comic hero and enemy models, cut-tone shading with
halftone and hatching, ink lines, dark indigo hard-shadow lighting, onomatopoeia on
hits. Combos keep the game's floating name label. The hero (the heretic, [heretic-hero.md](heretic-hero.md))
also faces the mouse and has a run for each direction. Enemies and dungeon pieces still fall back to
KayKit per model when their comic GLB is missing.

**None of the rendering has ever been seen.** It was built with `--headless` only, and
headless Godot neither compiles shaders nor draws. Run `tests/comic_shots.gd` first.

There is no switch any more: `--comic`, `--heretic`, `--comic-moves` and the Settings toggle are gone
(ROADMAP round 28), and the old `video/comic_style` key in `settings.cfg` is ignored.

## Where the art comes from

Models generated in Meshy take a different route: see [meshy-pipeline.md](meshy-pipeline.md).

`assets/comic/` is generated by the Blender scripts in `tools/comic_art/` (see its README). That
folder is a snapshot: the live copy is `vendor_src/shaman_redesign/comic_poc`, which is gitignored,
and the scripts hardcode that path. If you change the live scripts, copy them back into
`tools/comic_art/` in the same commit. The effects and reels in `comic_vfx.py` and `ability_vfx.py`
are Blender only; the game has just the words from `ComicFx`, and its casts keep their
existing look.

## Foe proportions (round 29)

The foes were built at realistic skeleton proportions and stood beside the chibi hero like another
artist's work. `tools/comic_art/chibi_pass.py` reshapes each finished enemy `.blend` without
re-rigging: same bones, names, weights and clips. Each bone gets an affine map of rest space:
`head` 1.5x about the neck (hoods, hats, horns and jaw ride along), hands 1.35x (and whatever they
hold), feet 1.3x, legs 0.62 along and 1.28 across, arms 0.9 / 1.18, spine and chest 0.84 / 1.1,
hips 1.08. A bone scales along and across its own axis, so its direction and every rotation key
keep their meaning; unlisted bones move rigidly with their parent; bones hanging to the floor
(robe hems, tails) keep their spread and squash in Z. Vertices move by their weights' blend of the
maps, everything drops until the feet are on z = 0 again, and root/hips location keys shrink by the
hip-height ratio. The archer's arrow is re-aimed from the nock every frame. Per-kind tweaks are in
`KIND` (mage hat, archer bow, shield, Warden cleaver and key chain, rogue and bomber legs).

**Tones in the hero's hand.** Line width was already the same (`Fx.COMIC_INK_PX` 1.6 on hero and
foes alike); foes look busier only because each part carries its own hull. The shadows differed:
the hero's is his paint times `PAINTED.P_HereticPaint.shadow`, a cool lilac at about half value,
while the foe tables shaded into deep warm browns. `ComicLook.material` moves every foe toon
material's shadow `FOE_SHADE_MATCH` (0.8 since the chunk pass; 0.5 first) of the way to `mid` times that tint (`foe_shadow`); the
hero, emits and dungeon pieces are untouched.

**The training dummy** is `assets/comic/dummy.glb` (`enemy_dummy.py`): a pale canvas sack tied off
at the top, red straps, a bullseye and boxing gloves, googly eyes, a buck-toothed grin and a plaster
cross. Clips: the skeletons' names plus `Dazed` (pupils spin, head lolls), which
`training_dummy._shake_off` holds over `DAZE_TIME`; every clip keys the eye bones' scale, so a
hit's squeezed-shut eyes never stick. Collision, the bag spring, the tag and the stars are unchanged.

**Chunky masses** (`tools/comic_art/chunk_pass.py`, after chibi_pass, reads `out/enemies_chibi/`,
writes `out/enemies_chunky/`). The skeletons were 30-130 separate bone islands, each with its own
outline in the game, so ribs and fingers read as busy line work beside the one-mass hero. The pass
sorts the bone mesh's islands into limb groups by their bone (head, jaw, torso, each arm, each hand,
each leg; anything under a weapon / shield / bow / staff bone stays as it was), fattens each group
along its normals (`FAT`: head 4 mm, jaw 8, torso 20, arms 30, hands 8, legs 36), voxel-remeshes it
(11 mm), smooths and decimates it (`TRIS`, at most 4x the group's old faces), and takes weights back
from the original group by nearest-face interpolation plus 4 smoothing passes, so elbows and knees
bend instead of shearing. Eyes, sockets, teeth, belts and helmets near the body move out with it
(`PUSH_R` 7 cm falloff). The torso keeps faint rib grooves (fat 20 mm, not 36) so it still reads as a
skeleton. Bone names, clips and the `g` attribute are kept.

**Health bars** sit at each kind's `bar_height` (`enemy_types.gd`, metres before model_scale: head
top in Idle_Combat + 0.3 m, measured on the chunky blends; the bomber clears its bomb), read by
`enemy_bars.bar_height()`; kinds without one keep `HEAD_HEIGHT` 2.4.

**Shadow tint** `FOE_SHADE_MATCH` 0.8 (was 0.5), judged side-lit (`foe_shots.gd -- --side`): bone's
shadow/mid ratio is then (0.43, 0.36, 0.50) against the hero's (0.42, 0.38, 0.56); 0.5 still read brown.

**Run strides.** The chibi legs cover far less per cycle than the game's move speeds (warrior 0.63 m/s
of planted-foot travel against 2.8 m/s, the shield's shuffle 0.11 against 3.2). Two fixes: chunk_pass
`KIND[...]['STRIDE']` widens a run clip's leg swing by scaling each leg bone's rotation about the clip's
mean pose, and the shield's walk is re-keyed (`enemy_shield.py`: stride .13 -> .3, 48 -> 24 frames);
then `enemy.gd` plays the run at ground speed / (`run_foot_speed` * model_scale)
(`run_clip_speed()`, clamped `RUN_CLIP_SPEED_MIN` 0.5 / `MAX` 3.0). `tests/foe_stride_check.gd`
(headless PASS/FAIL, in `agent.sh check`) samples both foot bones over each run clip, takes the mean
backward speed while a foot is moving back in the lower half of its height range, and fails if that is
8% off the stored number or the feet end more than 15% off the ground at move_speed. The minion's
shamble swings its feet low in both directions, so its forward swing still slides.

**Foe locomotion (round 38).** The runs above were the kit's KayKit-era clips widened and sped up (up to
3x). Since round 38 `tools/comic_art/foe_keys.py` re-keys every foe's run, and its Hit_B, Lie_StandUp
and Death_A, on the chunky blends, from per-foe specs in `tools/comic_art/foe_cycles.py`: legs by
two-bone IK onto ankle targets, a planted ankle sliding back exactly `move_speed / model_scale / 24` m a
frame, so every run plays at ~1x with its feet on the floor (`run_clip_speed` 0.96-1.03; `RUN_CLIP_SPEED_MIN
/ MAX` now 0.35 / 1.6). Legs, hips and the whole-body squash (root bone scale about the floor, feet
corrected) are keyed every frame; arms, head and torso turns are held on twos. Loops run 0..N with N == 0.

## How it works

**Stances.** The rig's rest pose is the hero pose, axe overhead and axe straight out, and offsets from it
kept that, so `abilities.py` now solves carries: `arm()` takes where the hand goes and where the axe points.
Both axes are `AXE_SIZE` and he drags them. Idle and the runs carry a `drag.l`/`drag.r` key instead of a hand
rotation: `keyed` aims the handle from where the hand really is that frame and `drag_rotation` leans the blade
so its tip lands on `TIP_Z`, so torso motion can't lift it off the floor (`comic_check` holds it within
`TIP_ON_FLOOR`). `Idle` is a 4 s loop of breath, weight drift and glances. `player._comic_stand` holds `Idle_Ready`, a low guard, for
`READY_HOLD` after an attack or hit, then relaxes, and plays `HERO_FIDGETS` in turn every `FIDGET_EVERY`.
A clip named `Hero_X` replaces the retargeted `X` at export: `Idle`, `2H_Melee_Idle`, `Hit_A`,
`Dodge_Forward`, `Jump_Start`, `Jump_Idle` are hand-keyed at their old lengths. The runs use the same carry.
`into_guard` eases each `Ab_` clip's arms from and to the guard over its first 4 frames and last quarter,
so hits must fall before that (`comic_check`). The old KayKit attacks, `Death_A` and the `Spellcast_` clips
still have retargeted arms.

**Element on the axes.** `ComicLook.infuse_axes` dresses the axe in `AXE_INFUSIONS`' blade and edge materials
(the hero's element is spirit, shown as wind's look through `HERO_AURA_AS`). `comic_axe_aura.gd` adds tongues along the edge, geometry stepped at 12 fps: `rise` flames stand up
in the world and stream behind a swing, `spin` gusts whirl round the blade, `shard` crystals stay fixed. Each
tongue is an outer colour on back faces round a hot core on front faces, so the core shows from any side.
Motes come from `comic_axe_aura._drip` (`DRIP_AMOUNT`). `charge` grows them while Awakened (`player._set_awakened`).

**Drag marks.** Each aura has a `comic_drag_mark.gd` it feeds the blade's tip every frame. Within `LIFTED` of
the floor the tip lays a point every `STEP_M`, and the points make a ribbon, an outer colour with a hot inner
line, in world space. It steps through the element's `stages` and thins as it ages: earth is magma cooling to
crust, wind a jagged electric cut that crackles while fresh (Tempest is the lightning path), water frost,
spirit a pale line. Lifting the blade or a jump over `JUMP` starts a new line. Attacks that touch the floor
leave marks too. The mesh is rebuilt every frame, capped at `MAX_POINTS` per axe.

**Mouse aim.** Only for the comic hero, and off for the bot, headless runs and once a gamepad was
the last device touched (then he faces the way he runs, as before). Free or charging, he faces the
cursor, read on a plane at `AIM_HEIGHT`. An attack from free snaps to it. A chained move turns at most
`COMBO_REDIRECT` as it starts, then keeps turning at `COMBO_TURN_WINDUP` until its first hit and
`COMBO_TURN_ACTIVE` after, which also steers its lunge. Moves started in the air and `AF` don't steer.
`ComicLook.run_clip` picks `Run_F`, `Run_B`, `Strafe_L` or `Strafe_R` from his travel relative to his
facing (`RUN_STICK` stops a flicker on diagonals). They share one 16-frame cycle, so a change of clip
keeps the step. They come from `run_clip` at the end of `tools/comic_art/abilities.py`; the retargeted
`Running_A` kept the hero pose's arms out about 2 m wide and is no longer played by him.

His rig must sit square in the GLB. The Blender reel keys the rig object to 78 degrees and clearing
its action leaves that behind, so `export_comic_shaman.py` zeroes it. `comic_check` fails if a
skeleton is turned in its model, which bone positions alone can't show.

The hero is `assets/comic/heretic.glb` (`ComicLook.HERETIC`; see [heretic-hero.md](heretic-hero.md)). The shaman model
`assets/comic/shaman_comic.glb` is no longer loaded by the game: it stays as the clip and weapon source
`tools/meshy_rig/heretic_bake.py` copies onto the heretic's rig. `L2`'s `Throw` is his `Ab_L2` (`CLIPS`).
`_effective_move` passes each id in `ComicLook.HERO_MOVES` (L1, L2, L3, DL, AL1) through `retime`, so its
hit lands on the clip's hit frame and the time after it stays as tuned. `MOVES` itself is never written.

### The base hero's design: the void arm

(Written for the shaman model the heretic replaced; the void arm's design carried over.)

One loud thing on him: his right arm is a purple void arm that shapes itself into his weapon. Earlier takes failed by
having too much going on (a half-transformed lopsided body; two elemental arms plus two axes): keep him simple.

- **Forms:** axe by default, dragged, with storm along it; hammer with lava and earth; spear with ice. They, E, and his
  smaller moveset are in [void-arm.md](void-arm.md). The hand for interacting with the world is still only a still:
  `tools/comic_art/unfinished_poc/concept_void.py`.
- **Body script:** `tools/comic_art/build_void.py` (the void arm, the left arm), on `build_tainted.py` (the shared body and its
  `K(mat, bones)` tags, read by the rig through `hint_of`: one bone pins, several bend as skin) and `build_comic.py`.
- **Void arm:** `void_arm` skinned softly across the right arm's four bones, `void_surge` the green lightning on it. It has no
  extra bones yet, so it bends where an arm bends rather than whipping. Storm is green (`P_StormGreen`).
- **Left arm:** a normal arm with air rings (`left_rings`). Its joints moved, and it holds nothing: `arm('l', ...)` leaves
  the hand alone and clips have no `drag.l`. Clips other than `Ab_L1` and `Ab_L2` still swing it as if it held an axe.
- **Axe:** `axe_void_*` on `hand.r`, parts `haft`, `blade`, `edge`, `core`. `HERO_AXES` has the one entry, so the trail, aura
  and floor mark are one each. Its aura and mark are wind's (`HERO_AURA_AS`), in its own materials.
- **The voice:** a thin vein from the rock ear to the lava eye (too small to see at game size), and `Idle_Listen`, which
  does the work: he tips his head to that ear, goes still, nods twice.
- **Budget:** about 41k tris, from 67k.
- **Look checks without Godot** (`tools/comic_art/unfinished_poc/`): `closeup_tight.py` each limb up close, at rest and
  mid-move (a broken arm doesn't show at 130 px, so always look here too); `clip_look.py` + `clip_sheet.py` one clip;
  `clearance.py` the axe against body and floor on every clip. They use stand-in materials, so only `comic_shots`
  confirms the real look.

Time after the hit is the same by construction; **the wind-up isn't**.

1. `main._set_model` asks `ComicLook.scene(kind)`: the GLB if present, else
   null and the KayKit model, per kind. A comic model sets `enemy.comic_kind`.
2. `enemy._ready`, for a comic model: `fit`, `apply`, and no KayKit shield, crossbow,
   bomb or tints, since the GLBs carry their own. Then `Fx.outline` as always.
3. `_play` runs each clip name through `ComicLook.clip`: the model's own clip if it has
   one, else `CLIPS`, where `""` means keep what's playing. The Warden's `Attack_Slam`
   contains the leap and landing, so `Jump_Idle` and `Jump_Land` map to `""`.
4. `_windup_anim` picks the Warden's clip per attack and sets its speed so the hit frame
   lands on the windup's end. `Block_Hit`, `Enrage` and `Bomb_Windup` use `_hold_clip`,
   since chase would otherwise replace them with run or idle next frame.

Dungeon pieces (`assets/comic/dungeon/`, built by `env_kit.py` to KayKit's names, sizes and
pivots) load through `ComicLook.piece`: comic if present, else KayKit, per piece.
`comic_*` materials set `tone_by_g`, so each stone's `g` multiplies its tone, and those in
`PIECE_INK_PX` carry a thin hull as `next_pass`. Floors and seams have none: their ink is geometry.

The toon shader's `light()` turns each light into a tone and keeps the brightest with
`DIFFUSE_LIGHT = max(...)`. `ALBEDO` is white, so that is the final colour; inner ink
sets `ALBEDO` to black. `EMISSION` carries the spot black and the rim, and
`ambient_light_disabled` keeps the palette exact. Dots and hatch use
`FRAGCOORD / VIEWPORT_SIZE.y`, a fixed number of cells per screen height.

## Sleek effects (round 33)

Zak, after playing every combo: "very comicy, which I like, but I'd like them to look a bit sleeker and more
put together. Right now it's very thick colours." The comic language stays (words, ink, flat tones, on-twos
stepping); what changed is weight. The target is a clean motion-graphics comic (Hi-Fi Rush, Persona 5 UI),
not thick crayon.

- **One palette per element** (`scripts/fx_palette.gd`): five tones each (ink, deep, body, light, hot) for
  wind, water, earth, void, spirit, the neutral "hit" and Awakened gold. `Fx.hit_spark` and `Fx.element_hit`
  take the blow's palette from the hero's move (`VOID_ELEMENT.ELEMENT_OF`, gold Awakened, else "hit"), so a
  storm string's stars, chips and bolts are one green family. Words and numbers keep their hot red-yellow.
- **Thin lines, not hulls.** Ink is a line of `PAL.LINE` (0.035 m, about 2 px at the game camera) round a
  shape, not a copy of it 1.2-2.1x its size. Applied in comic_pop (stars, chips, hoops), comic_bolt, comic_gale,
  comic_geyser, comic_fist, comic_comet, comic_crack, dark_fx (splat, spout) and the frost spear's disc.
- **Gradients and transparency instead of opaque fills.** A hit star is one shape, its light core fading to
  its body colour at 85% alpha at the tips (was three opaque stars nested in ink). The swoosh and weapon trail
  shaders draw a clear-to-tinted body (`body_alpha` 0.55 / 0.5; Awakened 0 / 0.18) with one crisp light line
  on the blade edge and a thin ink line past it. Bolts, gales, geysers, splats and the comet body are 0.85-0.9
  alpha with a narrower light core line.
- **Fewer shapes.** Stars at most 8 spikes with sharper valleys (`STAR_INNER` 0.3), chips 60% as many and
  75% as big, speed strokes 55% as wide with no ink, gale 4 bands (was 5) at 0.22 m (0.32), geysers 3 blobs
  (6), Maw / Collapse ink drops halved, two speed lines behind Molten Palm's fist (three ink wedges), frost
  plates 4 (7). `Fx.ring` drops a second hoop laid on the same spot within 40 ms (the colour + white pairs).
- **Shorter-lived.** Words 14 frames (20), numbers hold 0.38 s (0.5), geysers lose their stub step, comic
  cracks at most 0.9 s, dark pools' ink at most 1.1 s (`dark_fx.POOL_SHOW`; the binds still hold for the
  gameplay time).
- **Awakening and daze.** `Fx.beam` (the Awakening column) is 70% as wide at 0.32 alpha round a thin core
  and gone in 70% of the time; daze shows two 0.16 m stars (three at 0.2).
- **Smaller words.** `WORD_SCALE` 0.32-0.64 (0.42-0.84), breaker words 0.78 (1.0), outline FONT_SIZE / 9
  (/ 4), print offset 3 / 4 px (4 / 6); numbers `PIXEL` 0.0036 (0.0042), outline 12 px (26).

### Round 2 (the camera 34% further out, hero about 140 px at 1080p)

- **Round 1 at the new distance:** filmed `string_film`, middle 60% of each frame. Hit stars, speed strokes,

## Invariants

- `settings.cfg` never gains a comic key.
- `enemy.model_scale` stays as tuned, because it sizes collision. Resize art in `RIG_SCALE`.
- Every GLB goes through `tools/comic_glb.py`. Godot only reads `COLOR_0`, and the exports
  keep `g` in `COLOR_1`, so a plain copy pins every glow to its top tone. `comic_check`
  fails on this.
- Characters don't receive the sun's cast shadows (`receive_shadow` 0, set for non-`comic_*`
  materials): they still cast on the floor, but self-shadow on a small model reads as black blotches.
- Open flat patches must face the camera, because the game culls and Blender doesn't, so a wrong
  side never shows in a Blender render. `env_kit.face_out` sets them; `comic_check` fails on a
  floor stone facing down.
- `comic_*` material names are what `env_kit._export_mat` writes; the tones come from its
  `E_*` originals. `tone_by_g` must stay 0 on enemies, whose `g` isn't a tone.
- A material with no tones renders in `FALLBACK_TONE` with a warning, and fails `comic_check`.

## Tuning

- `ComicLook.TONES`, `EMITS`: palette, copied by hand from `comic_lib.py` and `enemy_*.py`.
- `ComicLook.KEY_*`, `FILL_*`, `AMBIENT*`: lights. The shader's bands are on the POC's scale,
  so changing `KEY_ENERGY` moves every band. `AMBIENT` isn't from the POC; it only lights KayKit.
- `comic_toon`: `DOTS`, `HATCH`, `rim_width`, band constants.
- Shadow texture, how heavy dots and lines are: `DOT_COVER`, `DOT_STRENGTH`, `HATCH_COVER`, `HATCH_STRENGTH` in `comic_toon`. Line and solid-black colour: `ComicLook.INK` (not `comic_ink`, a separate entry in `EMITS` set to match).
- Axe size: `AXE_SIZE` in `abilities.py`, then re-export. Marks: `LOOKS` in `comic_drag_mark.gd`.
- Carries: `DRAG_HAND`, `DRAG_HEAD`, `GUARD_*`, `COILED_*`, `STREAM_*`, `FLUNG_*` in `abilities.py`, then re-export.
- Stance timing: `READY_HOLD`, `FIDGET_EVERY` in `comic_look.gd`. Axe effects: `LOOKS` in `comic_axe_aura.gd`, `AXE_INFUSIONS` in `comic_look.gd`.
- Mouse aim: `AIM_HEIGHT`, `AIM_DEAD_ZONE`, `AIM_ASSIST_MOUSE_ANGLE`, `COMBO_REDIRECT`, `COMBO_TURN_WINDUP`, `COMBO_TURN_ACTIVE` in `hero_input.gd`.
- Ink at the game's camera, where the hero is about 130 px tall: `Fx.COMIC_INK_PX`, `COMIC_INK_WOBBLE`,
  `COMIC_INK_PUSH`, `ComicLook.PIECE_INK_PX`, `PIECE_INK_PUSH`, `INNER_SCALE`. Models are built from many
  parts with a hull each, so a wide hull with no push covers the model in black. The POC's own widths
  and `inner` values suit close-up renders only.
- `FILL_ENERGY` is above the POC's: below about 0.2, shadow sides drop under `spot` and go solid black.
- `ComicFx`: `WORDS`, `WORD_SCALE`, `WORD_GAP_MS`.

## Gotchas / open questions

Assumptions only a windowed run can confirm:

- The shaders compile, including writing a varying in `fragment()` and reading it in `light()`.
- `DIFFUSE_LIGHT` accumulates across lights, so `max` works, and `LIGHT_COLOR / PI` gives the
  energy on the POC's scale. If not, every tone band shifts.
- Inner ink and rim widths. The POC's rim is thinner than its inner ink so barely shows;
  `rim_width` puts a band outside the ink, guessed at 0.05.
- The brush streaks use skinned `VERTEX`, so they may crawl on moving limbs.
- The hull is a next pass; under a hard sun it may self-shadow into blotches (the POC hit this).
- First-use shader hitch: comic materials are not in `elements.prewarm` yet.

Lights and floor effects (read from the code, none of it seen):

- Torch `OmniLight3D`s are off. Toon tones take only a light's brightness, so a torch
  could only step the wall's tone, not warm it, and six lights cost every wall and floor pixel.
  Dropped rather than judged. `env_room.py`'s drawn warm shapes round each torch are not ported.
- Kept, can't judge headless: coloured lights from elites, projectiles, Awakening
  and casts. On toon they become a stepped brighter pool with no tint; on KayKit they still tint.
- The game has no `Decal` nodes. Scorch, telegraphs, rings and dust are unshaded alpha meshes, so
  they ignore the lights. Pieces match KayKit's floor height (y 0.05), so their offsets still clear
  it. Scorch's soot may vanish on the dark floor, and effects tuned for glow lose their bloom.
- `main._fade_occluders` sets `transparency` on pillars and rubble. Whether the hull fades with
  the toon pass is `29_pillar_faded.png`'s question.
- `env_room.py`'s shadow-only roof, which pools the light over the fight, is not ported.

KayKit assumptions the comic hero runs into:

- `tools/comic_glb.py` renames a mesh node sharing a joint's name: Godot gave the name to the mesh
  and imported the bone as `head_2`, so every `head` attachment would have silently landed at the
  origin. `comic_check` now resolves every attachment.
- The spirit casts' `axe_mesh` stays the KayKit Barbarian's `1H_Axe`. His weapons are skinned in
  place and can't be ghosted from an origin, hence `_axe_mesh`.
- `weapon_trail.gd` follows a mesh's transform; skinned axes never move theirs, so
  `ComicLook.hero_blades` rides invisible boxes on the hands. Direction from the axe head's bounds.
- `Fx.afterimage` bakes every skinned mesh. He's 67k tris in 29 meshes, so dash ghosts may
  cost far more than on KayKit. Unmeasured. He is also about 2.45 m to KayKit's 2.18 m, unscaled.

Known gaps:

- `Bomb_Windup` has planted feet but the bomber keeps chasing while lit, so it may skate.
- Not ported: the POC's 8% element-light tint, the inked line weights from the `inkw` group
  (the `wobble` sine stands in), `iron_extra` crack veins, spot-black from fill-only areas.
