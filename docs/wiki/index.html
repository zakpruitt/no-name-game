Awaken

A fast 3D combo hack and slash in a comic-book style. Right now it is one character, the heretic, in a training room full of punching-bag dummies: every string, breaker and counter he has, with nothing between you and practising them. No run, no title screen; the game boots straight into training.

The look is chibi but badass: big heads and hands, short stocky bodies, oversized weapons, flat bold colour under an ink outline, menace from the silhouette (a hood, a tentacle, glowing eyes) and never from detail. Every hit says something: inked onomatopoeia, a hit star, a stop.

The heretic in one breath

  • L swings a weapon, and the count picks it: one is the axe, two the spear, three the hammer.
  • H (right click) is the tentacle, and it does the weapon's element's thing: storm throws them up, ice turns them into objects, magma shoves them down and apart, dark (H with no weapon first) pulls them in and swallows them.
  • The big strings end in a breaker window: he leaps, time slows, and one press picks the finish.
  • Elements leave states on foes; a different element's hit cashes them in.

Mouse aims. Inputs in this wiki read like the game's combo text: L L H is light, light, heavy; Dash L H is a dash, then light, then heavy. A dash cancels out of anything. The combo guide beside him shows the next presses mid-string (Settings toggles it), and Esc opens the MOVES panel, laid out by the same slot groups as this wiki.

Breaker windows

When a big string connects he leaps up and back with his tentacles on what it hit, the game drops into slow motion and a ring closes round him. The chips under it show every ending: RMB is always there, LMB only when that window has a second ending. Press as the rings meet for a PERFECT (x damage, more Awakening). Too early locks it out; no press floats him down; a dash calls it off.

The counter

Dash into a foe's strike as it is about to land (its last s, aimed within °) and the dash becomes Backlash: the tentacle catches the weapon, marks the attacker and slams it. In the last s it is a perfect, and a window opens for Ricochet. A dash with nothing coming is just a dash, so pressing it costs nothing.

Awakening

Style rank (D up to SSS: variety beats mashing) fills the meter. Full, press Q: a burst blows foes back, and while it drains he hits x, takes x and can't be staggered, runs x, with a gold rim and a charged axe. Same inputs, stronger him. Esc → Fill Awakening to practise it.

The training room: four dummies that never die and walk home when knocked away, four pillars R swings round, and a damage readout for the current, last and best string. Each dummy shows a LAST tag of what its last string did. Turn on Settings → Dummies swing (or launch with --swing) and a dummy you stand in front of winds up and swings at you, so the counter has something to catch.

The rooms

Four training rooms, picked in Settings → Room (on restart) or with --room=plain|pieces|cistern|forge. Each room is data: its pieces, which of them are live, and the rules it adds. The environment is part of the kit: the heretic's elements have their own verbs on a room's pieces, and each new room has a combo that only works there. Hazards (kegs, live plates, spilled coals) hurt foes only.

Plain

The church's drill hall, swept bare: four pillars to swing on and torches on the wall. Walls and pillars still count: Pinwheel, Slapshot and Ricochet splat foes on them, and the bracket shows which one (put the cursor on another to switch).

With pieces

One of everything the prison was built from, each by a dummy: rubble, spikes, a powder barrel, a crate, a grate, a pool, a lit brazier, hooks. Powder Keg (Molten Palm into the barrel), Frost Wall (the spear on the grate), Live Floor, Uproot (a pillar torn out), and the flourishes: Gallows (Ricochet hangs a foe on a hook), Ice Rink, Brazier Spill. Used-up pieces come back 6 s later.

Flooded cistern & forge hall

Cistern (ice and dark): a channel of four pools along the back wall that freezes as one. Dash L H, pause, LMB by the west pool sends the cube row the whole channel, ~21 m, into the east wall. Forge (magma and storm): a powder line of three kegs between two braziers, where L L L H H bursts all three and tips both braziers; and bonded iron plates that go live together under Live Wire, for Short Circuit from every plate.

For live practice, --spar=minion|rogue|shield|warrior|archer|mage adds one real foe at a third of its damage that comes back when it dies: something to counter, block-break and parry bolts from.

Where it is going

Awaken is headed for roguelike runs: fights, rewards, a build that changes run to run. The heretic's kit was rebuilt (round 49) so that a run has stable things to upgrade, and so a second character can be picked up and played on the first try:

  • Upgrades target a slot or a window, never a route step: "Deep Freeze freezes 2 m wider", "Tempest lifts big foes too". The slots are the same on every character, so the upgrades are too.
  • Passives target an element or a state: "+20% frozen payoffs", "charged lasts 5 s". They carry across characters, because every character's elements map onto the same six states.
  • Unlocks come first from what was demoted: Storm Pitch returns as a Tow Line upgrade, and 18 richer element-matrix cells come back one at a time (see Elements & states).
  • More characters on the same card: each fills the same ten slot groups with the same inputs. The Dragon Prince is the first, in design (see Characters).

The kit grammar

Every character is one signature plus three elements, filling the same slots with the same inputs. Like Smash: pick up anyone and the pathways are familiar (L H always launches, H H always gathers, Dash into a strike always counters). When to use each is what makes the character.

The signature is the control tool that makes the character feel unique: the heretic's void tentacle grabs, marks, drags and conducts. The three elements are the damage colours, and each one owns a state it leaves on foes. A new element never adds a state, it maps onto one (a plant character's "rooted" would be bound; a blood character's "bleeding" would be molten).

Shared, element-free states: airborne and buried. A foe against a wall or pillar (wall-splat) is a position the moves read, not a state.

The six states, one rule each

Any element's hit on a foe in a state pays that state's rule. The rules are written once, for every character. States last s (charged s): dash out of one string and hit with another element.

The ten slot groups

The count is fixed; the moves, elements and best situations are the character's. Inputs: L light, H heavy, Dash, and the window's L / H. A window slot ends in a breaker window.

Rules of thumb

  • L H always launches. L L H always plants or slows. L L L H always carries or shoves.
  • H always marks. H H always gathers. hold H is always the one-big-target tool.
  • Dash L H always collects a crowd in front of you. Dash H H always pins one.
  • Slot 3's storm move wants a line, the ice one a stand, the magma one a wall. Which move is which is the character's.

Breaker windows: one or two endings

  • Windows sit on slots 3, 4, 5 (the last three), 7, 8 (finishers) and 9: at most 15 per character.
  • Each has one or two endings. RMB is the payoff you can always take; LMB is the environmental or situational one.
  • Two endings only when both have a clear best situation ("only when it's truly compelling"). Breakers are where a character gets to be plentiful and flashy; the slots stay fixed.

Crossovers: one per colour pair

Four colours (signature + three elements) make six pairs, so at most six crossovers. Each is a fixed extension off a move already on the card, two elements in one move: the first's state cashed by the second. The hits that only walk from a mid to a crossover (the heretic's Rockslide, Ice Cube, the vault) are route steps, not slots, and never an upgrade target.

Upgrades: what is gated today

  • moves and cells live in the game behind a Kit upgrade, off in the base kit:
  • Storm Pitch (Dash H L): a second dark x storm move beside Galvanize, so it is a Tow Line upgrade. Without it, Dash H L is Tow Line then Slice.
  • The 18 richer matrix cells (Gale Lift, Overload, Arc Flash...): off, the state's one rule plays; on, the cell plays in its place.
  • Runs will hand these out. Today --kit=full turns every one on, --kit=storm_pitch,overload named ones.

The counter and the swinging dummies

Backlash KRRAK-SNAP!

Dash at a foe in the last s of its swing, aimed within ° of it. He stops, turns and is untouchable through the strike; a hitstop and a violet parry flash (the tentacle curled round the weapon); the attacker is staggered and marked, swung up over him and slammed down in front for . + Awakening. A bolt in the window is batted back at its shooter for double damage.

Ricochet

A perfect counter (the last s) gives + Awakening and opens a one-ending violet window. RMB: the tentacle hurls the attacker at m/s into the nearer of the nearest foe and the nearest wall or pillar within m, for (x on a wall), held there s by tendrils. Best: a wall or a crowd near the attacker.

Swinging dummies

Settings → Dummies swing (or --swing). Stand in front of a dummy ( m, °) for s and it winds up with the same telegraph ring the sparring foes use ( s), its bag rocking back, then swings for ; again every s while you stay. Dash as the ring is nearly full.

Elements & states

Leave a foe in a state, then hit it with a different element: it pays the state's one rule. A dash out of any string into a move of another element does more than a plain hit.

The own-element exceptions

  • The element that leaves a state never pays its rule; its own move's thing plays instead: .
  • Some moves pay a state in their own body, so it never pays twice: Molten Palm on the frozen is its own steam (that is Steam Burst), Updraft Lash digs up the buried itself, and the storm air string and Tow Line's rip already juggle the airborne.
  • The skewered (Impale) are frozen too: they pay Shatter.
  • A foe in several states pays the rarest first; charged pays last, since nearly every Live Wire leaves it.

Changelog

Rounds 50-57

The room joins the fight. Combos pick a wall or pillar with a bracket you can move (round 50). The room became data with pieces and the heretic's verbs on them: Powder Keg, Frost Wall, Live Floor, Uproot, then Gallows, Ice Rink and Brazier Spill (rounds 51-53). Two new rooms, the flooded cistern and the forge hall, each with a combo only it allows (round 57). Four cool passes rebuilt every mid and ending so each has a best situation, and live spar foes to counter.

Round 49

The kit grammar. The heretic's moves were laid onto a fixed card of ten slot groups, the same for every future character; the MOVES panel and combo guide show each move's slot. New: the dash counter (Backlash, and Ricochet on a perfect) and dummies that swing so you can practise it. New crossover Lava Leash (L L L H L) with its breaker Hellseam, filling the last colour pair. The element matrix became six states, one rule each; the old cells and Storm Pitch became upgrades for later runs.

Round 48

Breakers got flashy. Three windows gained a second ending where it's truly better somewhere: Meteor Shower (scattered foes), Pinwheel (a wall nearby), Tempest (a crowd). Every ending now lands as one comic beat: an element-coloured impact frame, a burst behind the pile, floor cracks, pillars shaking dust, and one name-and-total banner.

Round 47

Harpoon (Dash L H) now gathers everyone round you into a frozen row, with two endings: Shatterstorm anywhere, Slapshot into a wall. Ice crystals gone: ice blocks fitted to each foe. Feel polish on the vault, the frost spear and Meteor Fall's wall splat.

Round 46

Ice and magma effects drawn by hand (Molten Palm's fist, lava, Undertow's pool, Meteor Fall's fireball), the Awakening's gold flame crown, and a filmed feel audit of every string with ten fixes.

Round 45

Quieter, drawn floor marks and breaker cues; the dark element's pools and black hole drawn; kills and dash streaks inked.

Round 44

A new heretic model, hand-built chibi with a real face. Storm made clearly storm: electric lime lightning, pale designed wind. Lightning runs along the tentacles (Live Wire's hang), Galvanize (H L L) zaps a whole crowd at once, the charged state, and Short Circuit as Grounded's second ending.

Round 43

The breaker window shows every ending you can pick (a chip per button in its colour). Effects stopped being polygons: authored, hand-drawn flipbook sheets for hit stars, slashes and the big landings.
