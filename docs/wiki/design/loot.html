Gear and boons

The two things a run gives you besides new moves. Gear is what the arm becomes. Boons are what the fight's rules become.

  • Gear is two slots you can see on him: the Graft (the tentacle arm) and the Shackle (the iron on his left wrist). Each piece names one move or weapon form and changes how it works: reach, speed, damage, or which state it leaves. You wear one piece per slot. A drop is a pick-up-or-leave choice you make standing on it, in a few seconds. Gear is lost when the run ends.
  • Three rarities: Iron (1 line), Hallowed (2 lines), Profane (1 line plus a rite that changes what a move does). No item levels, no inventory, no stash.
  • Boons: you pick 1 of 3 and keep it for the run. You never swap one or lose one. Each boon names a state, a window or a system (the breaker, the counter, the dash, the Awakening). It changes that rule for every move that touches it.
  • The first boon pool is nearly free. The game already has 18 stronger state payoffs (the old element cells, IMPROV_UPGRADE), switched off behind Kit.has(). Each one is a finished, judged boon. Turning one on is a data flip.
  • The test that keeps the layers apart (the hub's): if it adds something you press, it is a Technique. If it names a move or a weapon form, it is Gear. If it names a state, a window or a system, it is a Boon.
  • Build Boons first and Gear second. If time runs out, Boons alone still make a roguelike. Gear alone does not.

1. What each layer is for, and why two

A run already changes the heretic through Techniques and Attunements: which moves he has and which elements. Gear and Boons must not repeat that, and must not repeat each other. Each layer gets one job and one test.

Gear: how a move works

  • Job: the find. Something drops, you look, you decide. The Diablo moment, kept to seconds.
  • Names: one move (Reap, Molten Palm, Backlash) or one weapon form (axe, spear, hammer, the bare arm).
  • Changes: how far, how fast, how hard, which state the hit leaves. At Profane, what the move does.
  • Lasts: until something better drops. You can swap it, so it is how you change course.
  • Shows: on the arm and the wrist, all the time.

Boons: the rules of the fight

  • Job: the commitment. You pick 1 of 3 and keep it. It is the backbone of the build.
  • Names: one of the six states, the breaker window, the counter, the dash or the Awakening.
  • Changes: what happens after a hit, for every move that touches that rule.
  • Lasts: the whole run. Never swapped, never lost.
  • Shows: in what happens: a different payoff, a different word.

Why not split it as "Gear is numbers, Boons are rules"? With only numbers on Gear, every drop becomes a spreadsheet. Letting Gear change the state a move leaves (Dash Stab leaves frozen) turns a drop into a question about play: "do I want my lunge to freeze?" You can answer that in a second. It also feeds the Boons: a Graft that makes a move leave molten is worth more if you hold a molten boon. That is how the two layers work together without doing the same job.

One layer or two?

A. Boons only

Cheapest. The first 18 are already built. Every reward is a choice of three. Cost: nothing drops, he looks the same in room 1 and room 12, and it is not what Zak asked for ("Diablo weapon/looting").

B. Gear only, with rule-bending lines

One system to build. Cost: you can swap anything, so nothing is ever a commitment and builds never settle. Random rule bends are harder to balance than chosen ones. The compare grows, because every line matters.

C. Both, Boons first

Each does what the other cannot. Boons give a build that settles. Gear gives a find, a way to change course and a look. The extra cost is about a week on top of Boons: the floor card, two slots, a drop. Built in that order, Boons alone can ship if Gear runs late, and Gear is judged in a game that already has builds.

Lesson from the old run (docs/history/roadmap-rounds.md): its powers were sorted by button, levelled to 3, fused in pairs, boosted by synergy tags and rerolled at shrines. It added breadth and numbers before the combat was good. This page keeps one rule per boon, two ranks at most and only on number boons, no fusions (the crossovers in Techniques already are the pairs), and no rerolls.

2. Gear: the small loot game

Slots: two, both on the model

The heretic's design (docs/characters/heretic.md) bans ornaments: no buckles, no jewellery, no pouches. Only two things on him can change without breaking the silhouette. One is the arm (the brightest thing on him; "the arm is the character"). The other is the broken shackle on his left wrist. Those are the slots.

SlotWhere on himWhat it changesHow it shows
GraftThe tentacle arm and the weapons it growsThe light string, the enders and big moves, the signature moves (Reap, Singularity, Black Tide, Overwhelm), by move or by form (axe, spear, hammer, bare)A band on the arm's deep purple cuff in the rarity's colour. Profane also tints the weapon head it names.
ShackleThe iron band and two links on the left wristThe dash, Dash Stab, Tow Line, Backlash, Ricochet, the air dash, how fast the Awakening meter fillsThe band's metal changes: iron grey, then pale bone, then black iron with a crimson link.

Two slots

Both are already modelled. Two cards compare in one glance. Every drop is for one of two things you already know.

Three slots (add Horns, for the Awakening)

More drops matter. Cost: a third change on the hood, the part the design says must stay clean, and a third card to compare. Keep for later.

Rarities: three, no item levels

RarityLinesWhat it is
Iron (r-1)1 affixOne plain change to one move or form.
Hallowed (r-2)2 affixesTwo changes, usually two moves that work together.
Profane (r-3)1 affix + 1 riteA rite is fixed per item and changes what a move does outright. Each Profane is hand-made and named.

Items do not scale with depth. A room-10 Iron is the same as a room-1 Iron. Later rooms drop rarer pieces more often; how often is the run's call. This removes the worst spreadsheet in loot games: comparing a bigger number against a better effect.

Affixes

An affix is one line. It names a move or a form and changes one thing about it. Iron and Hallowed lines are drawn from the slot's pool, and a Hallowed never draws the same line twice. Each line has one fixed value ("+15%", never "+11 to 19%"), so two Hallowed with the same lines are the same item. A piece has at most three lines, counting a rite.

Graft pool (8)

  • Long Reach: Reap, Black Tide and Overwhelm reach +15%. C1
  • Quick Form: the light string (L, L L, L L L) plays 10% faster. C1
  • Heavy Head: hammer moves (Triple Chop, Molten Palm, Undertow) +15% damage. C1
  • Keen Leaf: spear moves (Frost Spear, Dash Stab) reach +20%. C1
  • Wide Crescent: Updraft Lash's gale radius +20%. C1
  • Many Tips: Harpoon takes 1 more foe (HARPOON_MAX 5 to 6). C1
  • Hard Landing: breaker landings off the big moves +15% damage. C1
  • Frost Edge: Dash Stab leaves the foe frozen for 1 s. C2

Shackle pool (8)

  • Quick Step: dash cooldown 0.3 to 0.25 s. C1
  • Long Step: dash distance +15%. C1
  • Iron Answer: Backlash damage +25% (18 to 22). C1
  • Wide Watch: Backlash's window 0.25 to 0.3 s; the perfect stays 0.1 s. C1
  • Hurled: Ricochet +25% damage. C1
  • Sky Grip: 1 more air dash per trip up (AIR_DASHES 1 to 2). C1
  • Tow Weight: Tow Line drags 1 m further. C1
  • Kindling: breaker landings give +20% Awakening. C1

Fifteen of the sixteen lines are one constant each. That is on purpose. The cheap lines carry the rarity system; the expensive work goes into a few Profane rites. (Cost classes C0 to C4 are in section 5.)

Drops and the compare

  • Where: the chest piece (already in env_piece.gd: heavy, shut or open) and, rarely, a foe. How many per room is the run's call.
  • On the floor: a Graft is a glowing knot of tentacle, a Shackle a dropped iron band, in the rarity's colour. It does not fly to you.
  • The compare: walk onto it and a floor card opens beside it. The new piece is on the left, what you wear in that slot on the right, three lines each at most. Lines that match are grey. A rite is in bold. Nothing else: no totals, no damage-per-second.
  • The choice: E swaps (the key is free since the old E move was removed). Your old piece drops where you stand, so E again undoes a wrong swap. Walk away to leave it. Whatever is on the floor is gone when you leave the room.
  • No inventory. You carry only what you wear. No salvage, no selling, no stash.
  • In a fight: the card stays shut while a foe near you winds up or while you are in a string. The piece waits.
  • Run end: all gear is lost. Whether a first Profane find is recorded anywhere is the run's call. This page recommends nothing carries over.

3. Boons: the power buffs

Signs: how boons are grouped

Every boon belongs to one sign. There are ten: the six states (bound, molten, buried, frozen, airborne, charged) and four systems (breaker, counter, dash, Awakening). The six state signs use the marks foes already carry (comic_state_tell.gd), so the icon on a boon is the mark you see on a foe.

By sign: states and systems

Matches how the fight is already read. You learn a frozen boon from frozen foes. Lets the offer show only what you can use.

By element

Overlaps Attunements, which already sort by element. Two layers would say the same thing.

By button

The old run's paths (Light, Heavy, Dash, Awaken). Pushes toward numbers on presses, not rule bends.

The offer

  • Three cards, take one. When and how often is the run's call. No reroll.
  • Three different signs in each offer.
  • Only what you can use now. A state boon is offered only if your current kit can leave that state. A payoff boon for an element (Gale Lift needs storm) is offered only while you hold that Attunement. A dark-and-magma run never sees a card it cannot trigger.
  • Lean in. Once you hold boons, at least one card in each offer is from a sign you already hold. Builds grow deep, not scattered.

Ranks and caps

  • Rule bends have one rank. You have it or you do not.
  • Number boons have two ranks. Taking one again makes it rank II, which doubles its step. After that it is not offered again. No rank III.
  • At most three boons per sign. A full sign stops being offered. Three deep in two or three signs is a build.
  • One payoff per state per element. This is already how the code works: improv_cell() picks one payoff for a state and the element that hits it. Two frozen boons for different elements both work. Two for the same element cannot exist.

Sample boons

Data-only means the behaviour, look and word are already in the game behind a Kit.has() id; the boon is an offer card that calls Kit.set_on(). New means code to write.

That is 22 samples: 9 data-only and 13 new. Of the new ones, 3 are number boons (C1) and 10 are rule bends on states and windows that already exist (C2). None needs a new effect sheet. Each reuses its rule's tell and word.

4. How the three layers make a build

Techniques and Attunements are defined on Simple kits. Each build follows them: two Attunements (each gives its ender and big move), and run.html's 5 Techniques, three of them the pair's crossovers. Each takes run.html's 5 Boons. The move names below are the game's today.

Thunder Cage

  • Attunements: storm, ice.
  • Techniques: the crossovers Galvanize, Impale and Hailstorm; Live Wire air (Worldbreaker); Short Circuit. (The Attunements gave Live Wire with Grounded, and Harpoon.)
  • Gear: Censer Chain (Graft, Hallowed), Loose Link (Shackle, Iron).
  • Boons: Overload, Superconduct, Long Thaw, Free Fall, Slow Bell.
  • How it plays: Harpoon the room, charge it with Live Wire, and one more storm hit sets the whole cage off at once; you fight crowds from range and never stand in them.

Gravedigger

  • Attunements: magma, storm.
  • Techniques: the crossovers Lava Leash, Galvanize and Fault Line (with Rockslide's buried as its step); Meteor Shower; Tempest. (The Attunements gave Molten Palm, and Undertow with Meteor Fall.)
  • Gear: Unconsecrated Hammer (Graft, Profane), Bellrope Link (Shackle, Hallowed).
  • Boons: Smother, Wildfire, Deep Grave, Meteor Spike, Aftermark.
  • How it plays: every Molten Palm starts a fire that spreads and every hit drives something into the floor, so you stand in the middle of the pile and hit each foe twice.

The Gallows

  • Attunements: ice, magma.
  • Techniques: the crossovers Impale (Anvil), Lava Leash and Steam Burst; Slapshot; Black Tide. (Dash Stab and Backlash are in the base kit.)
  • Gear: Knotted Cuff (Graft, Iron), Gallows Chain (Shackle, Profane).
  • Boons: Static Parry, Riposte Chain, Cold Step, Torn Off, Brittle Crowd.
  • How it plays: you wait for them to swing, answer every strike, hang the attacker and shatter the frozen ones round it; a duelist, not a brawler.

The three differ in where you stand (at range, in the middle, at the edge), what you press most (H holds, L L L H, the dash) and what wins a room (a chain, a pile, a counter). That is what "different characters" should mean.

Where Gear and Boons multiply, and how it stays sane

Unconsecrated Hammer x Wildfire

The Hammer makes Molten Palm leave molten on a line of foes. Wildfire makes each Burst leave molten on the foes its burn catches. Together: one palm, then any hit, and the fire walks across the pack.

Kept sane by: the code's rule that a state set during a move cannot pay in the same move (improvise() reads every foe before any pays), plus a per-foe wait of 1.5 s before Burst can fire on it again. It spreads one ring per hit, never the whole room in one frame.

Censer Chain x Overload

Harpoon taking 6 means 6 charged foes. Overload makes each zap harder for every other charged foe (OVERLOAD_PER 0.35 each), so the total grows with the count squared.

Kept sane by: a ceiling on Overload's multiplier (x2.4, five others), however many are charged.

Cuff of the Unshriven x Static Parry

Two counter effects both want to set a state on the attacker. They would fight.

Kept sane by: exclusivity. A boon and a Profane rite that change the same thing are never both live. The offer does not show Static Parry while you wear the Cuff. Take the Cuff off and it can be offered again.

Aftermark x Tithe Blade

Every breaker leaves a state, and the plain third light then pays it. Strong, and intended: the build learns to use its whole kit.

Kept sane by: nothing extra. A payoff spends its state, so it is one payoff per landing.

Stacking rules (one table in the data)

  1. Same number: add, never multiply. Gear and boon steps on one number (a reach, a hold, a window) add into one bonus. Two +15% reach is +30%, not x1.32.
  2. Every tunable has a ceiling. State hold at most +1.5 s. Breaker window close at most +0.12 s. Dash cooldown no lower than 0.18 s. Overload at most x2.4. Air dashes at most 2.
  3. Chains wait per foe. A boon that sets a state from a payoff (Wildfire, Brittle Crowd, Aftermark) cannot set it on the same foe again for 1.5 s.
  4. One writer per thing. A Profane rite and a boon that change the same move's result are never offered together.
  5. No damage-only boons. A boon that is only "+x% damage" is cut. Damage lives on Gear lines, capped by rule 1.

Do runs differ with that? The arithmetic

  • Gear per slot: 8 Iron + 28 Hallowed (8 choose 2) + 1 or 2 Profane = about 38 distinct pieces. Two slots: about 1,400 pairs.
  • Boons: a run ends with 5 boons (the run's count), and 16 choose 5 is 4,368 sets before the filter, still thousands after.
  • Attunements: a run holds two of three (kits.html), so 3 pairs, and the filter means each pair sees a different part of the boon pool.
  • What a player feels is not the count but the build's centre: its top two signs and its Profane, if any. 10 signs give 45 sign pairs. With 3 Attunement pairs and 4 Profane cases (none, or one of 3), that is over 500 recognisably different centres. Two runs in a row will rarely share one.

The floor: about 12 boons across at least 6 signs and 1 Profane per slot. Below that, offers repeat inside one run.

6. Reading it in the fight

The game already speaks three ways: comic words on payoffs, the combo guide on his right, and breaker names called out on landings. Gear and Boons use those, and add one strip.

  • The word tells you which boon fired. A cell boon already has its own word: Shard Cut is SHA-KRAK!, not Shatter's SHA-KRASH!. A new rule bend plays its rule's word with a small sign pip under it. You learn "that was my boon" because the word changed.
  • The combo guide tells you what a string will pay. It already tags a row "+ Shatter" from hero_moves.pays(). With a boon, the tag shows the boon's name instead ("+ Shard Cut", "+ Wildfire"). A move changed by a Profane rite gets a crimson dot by its name.
  • The strip: bottom left, one row. Two gear chips (Graft and Shackle, in their rarity's colour, a one-word name), then the boons as small sign icons grouped by sign, rank II drawn with a second ring. When a boon fires, its icon flashes once. Nothing else is added in a fight.
  • The pause page, one screen: "This run". Left column: Attunements and Techniques (kits.html's list). Middle: the two gear cards in full. Right: boons by sign, one line each. Every line names the move or state it changes, in the move list's words.

Decisions for Zak

  1. Two layers, with the test "names a move or form is Gear; names a state, window or system is a Boon"? Yes.
  2. How many gear slots? Two: Graft (the arm) and Shackle (the wrist).
  3. How many rarities, and do items scale with depth? Three (Iron, Hallowed, Profane). No scaling.
  4. Is gear lost when the run ends? Yes, all of it.
  5. Do the 18 existing upgrade cells become boons? Yes. They are the first pool and nearly free. They are Boons, not Techniques.
  6. How are boons grouped? Ten signs: the six states, and breaker, counter, dash, Awakening.
  7. Does the offer show only what you can use? Yes: no state your kit cannot leave, no payoff for an element you do not hold.
  8. Do boons rank up? Number boons to rank II only. Rule bends never.
  9. Rerolls? No.
  10. Which ships first? Boons. Gear after Boons are judged in play.

Risks

Not every state is reachable early. Today molten comes only from Lava Leash, Hellseam, Meteor Shower, Meltdown and Eruption, and none of them is in the magma Attunement. If the kit leaves out the moves that set a state, that sign's boons never appear. The filter hides the gap; it does not fill it. The fix is kits.html's proposal that Molten Palm leave molten (a decision on the hub); Unconsecrated Hammer is a partial fix until then. Early sign: count how often each sign is offered over ten test runs.
Some rule bends are invisible. Deep Grave and Long Thaw change timing, and a player may never notice. Every new boon must pass one test in play: can Zak tell it fired without looking at the strip?
Profane can outshine Boons. A Profane that defines a build makes the boon offer feel small. Keep Profane to one per slot and keep each rite on one move.
Chains are where feel breaks. Wildfire, Brittle Crowd, Aftermark and Overload all spread. The waits and ceilings are guesses until played in a full room.
The floor card in a fight. If drops land mid-fight, the card may never feel safe to read. "The card waits while a foe winds up" needs a feel check.
Words get noisy. Eight boons firing is a lot of comic words. If the screen clutters, cell words stay and rule bends use only the pip.

Not yet

A third slot (Horns, for the Awakening). It touches the hood, the part that must stay clean. Add it only if two slots feel thin.
Profane weapon head models. C4 each. A tint first; a mesh once the item has proved itself.
Sets, crafting, salvage, selling, a stash, item levels. The spreadsheet half of loot games. It is what "simpler" leaves out.
Rank III, rerolls, curses, trades. The old run had them. They added choices about numbers, not about play.
Gear that carries between runs. The run owns what carries. This page recommends none.
Room-piece boons. gallows, ice_rink and brazier_spill are Kit ids already, but they only work in rooms with those pieces. Add them when the run's rooms carry the pieces reliably.

Build order

The master order is run.html's ten slices. These steps are this page's detail inside three of them: run slice 4 (Boons), run slice 5 (Gear) and run slice 8 (the full run).

  1. The offer, six cells

    A boon card screen (3 cards, take 1) that calls Kit.set_on(). Six data-only boons: Shard Cut, Gale Lift, Overload, Superconduct, Meteor Spike, Torn Loose. Until the run's rooms exist, open it from a debug key in training. Judge: does picking one change how you play the next minute?

  2. Signs and the filter

    The ten signs, "only what you can use", "lean in", the guide's "+ boon name" tag and the strip. Judge: can you say what you hold without pausing?

  3. Four rule bends

    Aftermark, Free Fall, Cold Step, Static Parry. Judge each alone, then all four together.

  4. Graft only

    Iron and Hallowed, the 8 Graft lines, the chest drop, the floor card and the E swap. Judge: is pick-up-or-leave under three seconds?

  5. Shackle and two Profanes

    The Shackle lines, Unconsecrated Hammer, Cuff of the Unshriven, the exclusivity rule. Judge: does a Profane change the run without drowning out the boons?

  6. Fill the first pools and the ceilings

    16 boons, 3 Profanes, the stacking rules in one data table. Judge with three full runs, each aiming at one of the three builds above.

Data shape when it is built: one table (a LOOT dictionary beside Kit.UPGRADES), one row per boon and per affix, each with its sign or slot, cost class, rank step and ceiling. Like RULES and BREAKERS, the tuning lives in the table.
