The run

How one roguelike run of Awaken goes: its rooms, its pace, when each layer of a build is offered, what the fights must ask of the kit, and what carries over. Most of all: the smallest run you can play this week, and the order to grow it in.

Build the run in thin slices, and judge each one by playing ten runs. The first slice is three rooms of the foes we already have, with one pick between rooms from upgrades that already exist behind Kit.has(). No map, no currency, no meta, no Gear. It answers one question: is the heretic fun against a live crowd?

The finished run: three floors of four rooms plus a warden fight, about 25 minutes. Two doors after every room, each showing the reward behind it. The first reward of a run is always an Attunement. A run holds 2 of the 3 elements, so it has 3 of the 6 crossovers. The kit gains at most one new idea per room, and nothing a run adds is longer than 5 presses.

Fights must keep foes alive long enough for a string to finish. Today a 75 hp warrior dies to the first breaker of most strings. So: crowds of 4 to 6, mixed roles, and core foes that survive one full string. Difficulty rises by mix and pressure, not by health.

No currency until late, and maybe never. Between runs, unlock items into the pools; no permanent stat boosts. The bone altar does not return. No map, no daily runs, no second character until the run is good.

The run we had, and why it went

Rounds 1 to 23 (docs/history/roadmap-rounds.md) built a whole roguelike in about five days, on the old KayKit shaman. Round 28 deleted it on Zak's call: "remove the roguelike layer, keep the training ground." The cleanup audit's deletion plan counts about 2,900 lines for the run flow and 1,300 for the powers.

What it had

  • 8 rooms, then an 8-column map with 3 lanes and the boss at the end.
  • Room kinds: fight, elite, rest, shrine, vault, trial, unknown. Seven kinds, plus a boss.
  • Elite twists (Hasted, Armored, Volatile, Splitting), two at once from room 6.
  • About 25 powers in 5 colour paths, with masteries, fusions, 7 synergy tags, levels to III and rerolls.
  • Bones as currency, spent at camp, shrine and reroll, banked at a bone altar for permanent boosts.
  • Hero levels and XP, and an Ascendancy (Wind, Earth, Water) at level 6.
  • Seeded runs, daily runs, run records, recent-run replay. One boss, the Bone Warden.

What it got right

  • Pick 1 of 3 on a steady beat. Easy to read, easy to tune.
  • Choosing the next room by its reward. Vaults let you route toward a build. That is real steering.
  • Elites as risk for reward, priced in plain words on the door.
  • Measuring pace. --pace found a plain room took the bot 16 s and an elite about 23 s, and caught Volatile being a free wipe.
  • A run seed kept apart from combat randomness (run_rng), so two runs could be compared.
  • Counting procs. It found four powers that never fired at all.

What made it worth stripping

  • It was built on a hero that went away. Powers hooked the shaman's moves (L1, Ground Breaker, Charged Slam) inside _do_hit. When the heretic replaced him, every hook pointed at nothing.
  • It was wide before the combat was good. Every "feel pass by hand" line stayed unchecked: powers, map, Ascendancy. The bot verified that things ran. Nobody judged whether they were fun.
  • Seven overlapping ways to grow. Powers, paths, masteries, synergies, fusions, levels and the Ascendancy all stacked on one hero. No single one could be felt.
  • The complexity needed explaining. Round 23 spent about ten items on panels that explain choices: mystery odds, "4 to 6 powers from here to the boss", vault contents, trial payouts. When a choice needs a panel, the choice is too complicated.

What must be different this time. The run grows the combat that is already polished: its rewards are the heretic's own moves and states, not a new pile of powers. Rewards target the kit grammar's stable slots and states (docs/characters/kit-grammar.md section 7), so a move change never orphans them. Each layer has one way to grow. One layer is added per slice, and Zak plays it ten times before the next. If a choice needs a panel to explain it, cut the choice.

  1. RoomA fight.
  2. RewardThe one its door showed.

Length: about 25 minutes, 15 fights

Start from how long a string takes. The autotest plays about 46 to 49 strings in about 3 minutes, so a string with its breaker is roughly 4 s including the walk up. A room of 8 to 12 foes, where fodder dies inside one string and core foes take two, is about 15 to 20 strings plus dodging: 60 to 90 s. The old game's bot cleared a plain room in 16 s with a far simpler hero. Ours should be slower, because the strings are the point.

A floor

A floor is four rooms, then its warden. Rooms 1 to 3 offer two fight doors (one may be the floor's elite). Room 4's doors are always rest or elite. Floors differ by foe mix, room layouts and pressure (see Fights), not by art. One biome, the church's prison, for the whole first game. The rooms already say where they are: the drill hall, the flooded cistern, the forge hall (docs/design/environment-lore.md).

The boss question

There is no boss today. The Bone Warden (443 lines, the shaman era) was deleted in round 28. A real boss is the single most expensive thing on this page: its own model, attacks, telegraphs and phases, all tuned by hand.

Wardens from parts, one real boss later

Floors 1 and 2 end with the Turnkey: an outsized skeleton warrior (scale 1.6, poise 30, hp x8) that a launcher does not lift, with escort waves. That costs data, not a model. It tests the pin slot (hold H, Overwhelm), Impale and the counter, which the dummies never test because nothing on the training floor is big. The final warden is one real boss, built in slice 8 once a floor plays well.

A real boss per floor now

Three bosses before we know a floor is fun is the old mistake again. Cost: three models, three attack sets.

No bosses

The run ends after the last room. Cheapest. A run with no climax feels like a practice set.

How a run ends, and what death costs

A run ends when the hero dies or the final warden falls. Either way he is back in the training hall, which becomes the hub: it already exists, it already has the moves panel, and a door out of it is the start button. Death costs the whole build: every Technique, Attunement, Gear and Boon. Nothing else is lost, because nothing else is kept (see Between runs). There is no consolation reward for dying.

The reward cadence

A run build has three layers: Techniques (including Attunements), Gear and Boons. What each one is lives on kits.html and loot.html. This page says when and how each is offered.

The rules of the cadence

  • One new idea per room, at most. Only Attunements and Techniques add presses. Boons and Gear never do, so they can stack freely. An Attunement's ender and big move count as one idea, because the big move is the ender plus one more H (ice is the exception: its big move, Harpoon, is off the dash; see kits.html).
  • Techniques are 1 of 2, and skippable. Small, because each is a whole move to learn. Skipping gives a small heal (15%), so a player who is not ready for a new input is not punished for it.
  • Boons are 1 of 3. They are read, not learned, so a wider choice is cheap.
  • Gear is a drop, not a menu. It falls in the room, you see it beside what you wear, you keep one. That is the loot moment, kept to one comparison.
  • The first Attunement comes first. It is the reward of room 1, before anything else pays out. It is what makes the kit grow, and it decides which crossovers this run can ever have.
  • Fourteen rewards a run (twelve rooms and two floor wardens; the final warden ends it). The base kit of 8 slots ends a run at about 16: still well under today's 24.
  • Input length. The base kit caps at 3 presses. Anything a run adds caps at 5. The 6-press Hailstorm into Meltdown stays parked (kits.html).

Steering: choose the next room by its reward

After each room, two doors. Each door shows one icon: Technique, Boon, Gear, Elite (Gear, one rarity up) or Rest. The two doors never show the same layer. That is all the steering there is, and it is enough: a player who wants more ice moves walks through Technique doors after attuning ice. No map. The old run went from two doors to a map in one round, then spent a round explaining the map.

Techniques are drawn only from moves whose elements you hold, so a Technique door after an ice Attunement is a door toward an ice build. A crossover enters the Technique pool the moment you hold both of its elements, and it is always in the next Technique offer.

Fights that need the kit

The combat was tuned on four dummies in a fan that never die, and on one spar foe at a time. A run is crowds of live foes that die. Two facts from the spar audits frame everything: at 1x hp the warrior (75) died in 37 of 49 strings, mostly to the string's first breaker, and a Ricochet onto the gallows (146 to 234) killed it on the splat. So in a real fight the holds, the hang and the Snap only happen to something big. The fights must fix that.

Crowd size and time to kill

  • 4 to 6 alive at once (cap 6; the old run allowed 8). The kit was tuned on a fan of four, and Gather, Harpoon and the Arc rule's 6 m want a cluster. Past six, the camera and the strings both lose him.
  • 8 to 12 per room, in 2 or 3 waves. The next wave steps in as the last one thins, so there is always someone to carry a state to.
  • Fodder dies inside one string. That is the crowd: it makes Arc, Burst and Gather worth doing.
  • Core foes survive one full string and die to the second. That makes a breaker land on something alive and a hold matter. The spar's HP_SCALE 3 on melee kinds already found this number; the run adopts it.
  • At most 2 melee windups at once (today's max_melee_attackers), so a string is broken by a telegraph he could have countered, not by a pile.

How difficulty rises without only inflating health

  1. The mix. Floor 1: minions plus one core kind. Floor 2: two core kinds plus a shooter. Floor 3: flankers and a shooter screen together, so standing still to finish a string is a choice with a price.
  2. Pressure. Melee windups at once: 2, 2, 3 by floor. Windup time x1.0, x0.92, x0.85. Always counterable: spar_check's 0.2 s of telegraph before the counter window stays a floor.
  3. Elite traits that switch off one easy answer. One trait per elite, never two. Each points at another tool in the kit: Rooted* (a launcher does not lift it: pin it, bind it), Skittish* (dodges like a rogue: snare it), Warded* (blocks from every side until launched or grabbed). A trait never makes a state impossible, because that would make a whole build dead for a room.
  4. Health last. At most x1.0, x1.2, x1.4 by floor, so a late string still feels like the same string.

The environment and the two data rooms

Rooms are already data (rooms.gd ROOM_DEFS). A run room is a ROOM_DEFS entry with rebuild false, which the code already calls a real room: what Uproot tears out stays out for the fight. Hazards stay hero-safe, as decided in round 57.

  • The pool of layouts: plain, with pieces, the flooded cistern, the forge hall, plus 4 new layouts made from the same fourteen pieces. A new layout is a placement list, no code.
  • Floor 1 draws the plain and with-pieces layouts. Floors 2 and 3 add the cistern and the forge. Their showcases ("down the channel", "powder line", "bonded plates") become the run's discoveries: combos only that room allows.
  • A room favours a pair. The cistern rewards ice and dark, the forge magma and storm. Later a door can show its room's piece family, so a storm build walks toward the forge (the lore page's "room modifiers on doors"). Not before slice 6 has shown that doors steer.
  • Flourishes are Boons, later. Gallows, Ice Rink and Brazier Spill are Kit.has() ids today. In a run they are Boons, but they wait in loot.html's Not yet until the run's rooms carry their pieces reliably. The training hall keeps playing them for practice.

Unproven, and only learnable by playing:

  • Whether the strings hold up with 4 to 6 live foes attacking. Every string was filmed on a fan of four dummies.
  • What a hit mid-string should do. The spar audit's open question (does a hurt reset the chain?) becomes the run's most important rule.
  • Whether the camera frames a crowd plus a breaker hang. Meteor Fall already pulls out to 26 m for one throw.
  • Every hp number above. They come from one spar foe, not a wave.
  • Frame cost with six foes and the heaviest crossovers at once. The old [perf] line had 17 foes on a much lighter hero.
  • Whether the counter gets used when four foes swing, or players just dash away.

Currencies and economy

None at first, then at most one

Every reward through slice 8 is a door and a pick. If a currency is added, it is tithe, the church's coin: dropped by elites and wardens, and paid out for Gear you leave behind. It lives only inside a run and is spent only in Rest rooms, on a stall of 3 things (a Boon, a Gear, a reroll of the stall). Cost: one HUD counter, one drop, one stall screen.

One currency from the start

Every room then pays out coin as well as its reward, and every price is one more number to tune before we know the run is fun.

Two currencies

One per run and one banked between runs: the old bones and altar. That is the meta tree below, and it doubles every balance question.

Shops: no shop room. The Rest room's stall is the shop, and it competes with the heal, so spending is a choice and not a chore. Add tithe only if ten runs of slice 8 show that leaving Gear behind feels pointless, or that nothing rewards playing a room well. If neither happens, ship with no currency at all.

Between runs

A. Nothing but knowledge

Every run starts the same. Cost: nothing. Risk: a new player who cannot clear floor 1 has nothing to show for a lost run, and every pool is open from run one, which is a lot to read.

B. Unlock items into the pools

No permanent stats. Pools start at about 60% and grow by deeds: "clear floor 1" adds a Boon set, "land Avalanche in a run" adds a Technique, "beat the Turnkey with ice attuned" adds a Gear base. Cost: an unlock list (data), a save file, one board in the training hall. It also onboards: the first runs offer fewer, simpler things.

C. A full meta tree

A banked currency buys permanent hp, damage, rerolls. Cost: a second economy, every fight balanced with and without the tree, and grinding in place of getting better at the strings. Wrong for a game whose point is the strings.

  • The bone altar does not return. Permanent stat boosts make the game easier by playing longer, not by playing better. If new players need help, give them a difficulty setting once the game is complete, not a currency.
  • When unlocks arrive: slice 10, after a full run exists. Until then every pool is fully open, so Zak sees everything while tuning.
  • New Techniques, Gear and Boons enter by the same list. A new item ships locked behind a deed that makes the player meet it on purpose.
  • Future characters unlock the same way ("win a run") and each brings its own Technique pool. Boons keyed to states ("+20% frozen payoffs") work for every character. A second character is far down the build order (see How it grows).

Variety and seeds

With small pools, run two must differ from run one by its choices, not by its content count. The arithmetic, with the pool sizes assumed in Assumptions:

  • The element pair. 2 of 3 elements in some order: 6 element paths, 3 distinct pairs. Each pair has exactly 3 of the 6 crossovers: dark with each held element, plus the pair's own. Storm and ice get Galvanize, the dark x ice crossover and the ice x storm crossover. Magma and storm get Galvanize, Lava Leash and Fault Line. This is the biggest difference between runs, and it costs nothing new: the moves exist.
  • Techniques. 5 picks of 1 of 2: the pair's 3 crossovers, and 2 from the 8 to 10 others the held elements allow. Two runs on the same pair still end with different moves about half the time.
  • Boons. 5 picks of 1 of 3 from loot.html's first pool of 16. Two runs share fewer than two Boons on average.
  • Room order. Layouts from 8, foe mixes from the six kinds by floor. Cheap variety, but texture, not identity.

The rule: a run is different because the player chose differently, and the doors let them. Random rolls alone give noise, not builds.

Seeds and daily runs

A seed from slice 1, no daily runs

One run_rng for doors, offers, layouts and spawns, apart from combat randomness, exactly as the old run did it. A --seed= flag makes a run repeatable for the bot and for comparing tuning. Cost: one RNG and one flag.

Daily runs now

They need content worth replaying every day and somewhere to compare scores. Neither exists. The old game had them by round 11, which says more about its pace than its needs.

The thinnest playable slice, and the build order after it

Each slice adds one thing. Each is judged by Zak playing it ten times, not by the bot. The bot keeps its job (nothing crashed, the strings still land), but it cannot say whether a run is fun. Each slice names the question it answers and the result that should change course.

  1. Three rooms (this week)

    What: a --run flag, no menu. From the training hall into three real rooms in a row: with pieces, the forge, the cistern, each with rebuild false. Each room is two waves of existing foes: minions and one core kind, then a mixed wave with a shooter. Melee core kinds at the spar's 3x hp. Today's full kit. Between rooms, pick 1 of 2 from the 19 ids already behind Kit.has(): Storm Pitch and the 18 matrix cells. (The 3 flourishes wait for rooms that carry their pieces.) Death, or room 3 cleared, returns to the hall. A seed. Nothing else: no doors, no Gear, no currency, no meta, no warden.

    Question: is the heretic fun against a live crowd, and does one pick change how room 3 plays?

    Change course if: across ten runs he mostly mashes the light string or dashes away; a hit mid-string breaks more than about one string in three; the camera loses him in a crowd; or the picks go unnoticed. Then the next work is combat against crowds (foe pressure, the hurt rule, the camera), not slice 2.

    Measure: room time, strings and breakers per room, hurts mid-string, deaths per room. One [run] line, like the old --pace.

  2. The small kit and Techniques

    What: start from kits.html's base kit of 8 slots. The picks become Techniques: 1 of 2, one per room, skippable. Until slice 3 adds Attunements, a debug flag holds two elements (kits.html's --attune=) so the element Techniques can be offered.

    Question: does room 1 feel complete with the small kit, and does one new idea per room feel like growth?

    Change course if: room 1 feels empty (put a slot back into the base), or new Techniques go unused in the next room (fewer, louder Techniques).

  3. Attunements

    What: room 1's reward is always an Attunement. A run holds 2 elements. An Attunement gives the ender and its big move; each crossover is a Technique, offered once both its elements are held. Five rooms.

    Question: do two runs with different pairs feel like two different characters?

    Change course if: Zak always picks the same element (it is stronger, or the others read worse), or the unattuned room 1 is dull (then start each run with one element held).

  4. Boons

    What: loot.html's first Boon steps (six of the old cells, then the signs, then four rule bends), bending the states, the breaker window, the counter, the dash, the Awakening. Picks alternate Technique and Boon.

    Question: does a Boon change what he does, or only a number on screen?

    Change course if: Boons are taken and forgotten. They are too quiet: cut to the loudest five, each with its own visible effect.

  5. Gear

    What: Gear drops from fights, with loot.html's few slots and short affix lists. Wear it or leave it.

    Question: does Gear give something Boons do not: the "a drop!" moment and a comparison worth making?

    Change course if: taking Gear feels the same as taking a Boon. Then merge the two layers (decision 10).

  6. Two doors

    What: after each room, two doors, each showing its reward layer. Elite doors.

    Question: does the player steer toward a build?

    Change course if: door picks look random. Then the layers are not different enough to want one over another. That is a pool problem, not a door problem.

  7. One floor and the Turnkey

    What: four rooms, the rest-or-elite last door, the Turnkey from parts, the difficulty rules above. About 9 minutes.

    Question: is one floor a satisfying session, and does the Turnkey make the pin, Impale and the counter matter?

    Change course if: the Turnkey dies to the same string as everything else. Then the warden needs its own answer before any real boss is built.

  8. Three floors and one real boss

    What: the full run. Floors 2 and 3 with their mixes, the cistern and forge in the pool, one final boss with its own model and attacks.

    Question: does the build carry the whole run, and is the end a climax?

    Change course if: floor 3 feels like floor 1 with more hp (the mix and pressure are not working), or runs are decided by floor 1's picks.

  9. Tithe and the stall (only if needed)

    Question: does a currency make leaving Gear behind and playing well feel rewarded? Change course if: the stall goes unvisited. Remove it and ship with none.

  10. Unlocks between runs

    Question: do the first runs feel simpler, and does a deed make a player try a new item? Change course if: players ignore the deeds. Unlock by floors cleared instead.

How the game grows from here

After slice 10 there is a small complete game: one character, one biome, three floors, one boss, small pools. What it costs to grow, cheapest first:

The order is the table's order. A second character is the biggest multiplier and the most tempting. It is also exactly the "wide before good" trap, one level up.

Risks

The kit may not survive a crowd. It was built against still dummies. If slice 1 says no, the run waits and combat work comes first. That is the slice doing its job, not a failure.
The run outgrows the combat again. Agents can build a slice a day; Zak can judge one every few days. The build order only works if each slice waits for its ten runs.
A pruned base kit may feel thin in room 1. The Attunement in room 1 is the hedge; slice 3's fallback is to start with one element.
Small pools repeat. By run ten Zak will have seen every Boon. That is fine for judging feel. It is not fine for players, which is what unlocks and later pool growth are for.
Gear and Boons may blur. Both are passive and add no inputs. If players cannot tell them apart, one layer is wasted polish.
The bot will say everything passes. It always did. Feel is judged by hand, and every number here is a placeholder until then.

Decisions for Zak

  1. Is slice 1 (three rooms, today's kit, picks from the 19 existing Kit.has() ids) the next thing to build? Yes. It is buildable from what the repo has, and it tests the combat against crowds before any run layer exists.
  2. How long is a full run? Three floors of four rooms plus a warden, about 25 minutes. One floor first.
  3. How many elements does a run hold? Two of the three, plus the dark signature, so a run has 3 of the 6 crossovers.
  4. Is the first reward of every run an Attunement? Yes, in room 1. If the unattuned room 1 is dull, start with one element instead.
  5. Map or doors? Two doors, each showing its reward layer. No map.
  6. Do core foes get the spar's extra hp in a run? Yes: warrior and shield 3x, rogue 1.5x. Fodder and shooters stay at today's hp.
  7. Boss? Floors end with the Turnkey, a warden from parts. One real boss at the end, built in slice 8.
  8. Currency? None through slice 8. Then at most one (tithe), spent only in Rest rooms, and only if playing shows a need.
  9. Between runs? Unlock items into the pools by deeds. No permanent stat boosts. The bone altar does not come back.
  10. If Gear and Boons feel the same in slice 5, merge them? Yes. Two layers that feel alike are one layer polished twice.
  11. Does the training hall become the run's hub? Yes. It exists, it has the moves panel, and a door out of it is the start button.
