The world builds back

One of four "unique spin" lanes. This one puts part of the build outside the hero: in the prison's rooms, its pieces and its foes. The second half borrows structures from outside action roguelikes.

Recommendation: Breaches, with Writs as the wardens' reward. One card in every Boon offer is a Breach: a crack in the prison, not a change to the heretic. Take it and every later room in the run is built differently. "Powder Store" puts a powder line of kegs in every room. "Rusted Chain" hangs two hooks in every room and turns on the gallows.

A Breach brings its own pieces with it, so it is never dead. It is offered only if the held spirit (or the dark) answers those pieces. Under the hood a Breach is one room key that the run keeps and merges into every later room. The keys are the ones rooms.gd already reads: a layer, links, flourishes, rebuild. Most Breaches are C1.

A floor warden does not give a Boon offer. It gives its Writ, the room rule its fight was built around. The Turnkey's room rebuilds broken pieces. Beat him and every later room does too.

Why this one: the spirit made the hero's growth feel like Awaken. Breaches do the same for the run's growth, and they use the most finished thing in the game besides the strings, the pieces and their verbs. The other Boons, the Gear lines and the doors stay conventional.

The idea in one paragraph

The prison was built to hold the four powers, so every piece is half a conductor (docs/design/environment-lore.md). The heretic's moves already act on pieces: Molten Palm carries foes into a keg, Frost Spear on a grate raises a Frost Wall, Overwhelm's Uproot tears out a pillar, a perfect Ricochet hangs a foe on a hook. Today those pieces sit in training rooms. In a run they can be what you build. You choose what the next rooms contain, you take rules off the wardens, and how a foe dies decides what it leaves. The hero's kit stays small. The room grows instead.

Scope rule for this page: no new effect sheets, no new hero states, nothing that makes a piece hurt the hero (round 57's hero-safe call stands). Every idea names the already-built verb it leans on. The old run was cut for being wide before the combat was good, so each proposal says what it replaces.

1. Breaches: Boons that crack the prison

Hook: some Boons change the prison, not the heretic, and every room after that is built differently.

How it works in play

  • The card. Every Boon offer of three holds at most one Breach. It reads like a Boon card, with a room icon instead of a sign icon.
  • What it changes. One room key, kept by the run and merged into every later room's ROOM_DEFS entry. Three kinds: add a layer (a small placement list, the shape a room already uses), turn a rule on (links, flourishes, rebuild), or change a piece's starting state (the state field place() already takes).
  • It brings its pieces. A Breach that turns on a flourish also adds the pieces the flourish needs. This is what moves the three flourishes out of loot.html's Not yet, where they wait "until rooms carry the pieces reliably".
  • The filter. Offered only if the held spirit, or the dark, answers its pieces (env_piece.usable()). Pillars and hooks are dark, so those Breaches suit every run. A keg Breach needs magma. A grate Breach needs storm or ice. Pools answer every element (water, frozen, live, steam).
  • Caps. At most 3 Breaches a run, and a layer never lands on a tile that is taken (the rooms check's overlap rule). Breaches never stack the same key twice.

The first pool

Powder Store

Layer + flourish. Magma. C1

Every later room places the forge's powder line: three kegs 3 m apart between two lit braziers. brazier_spill on. Molten Palm into any keg walks the whole line.

Rusted Chain

Layer + flourish. Dark. C1

Every later room hangs two hooks on pillar faces, and gallows is on. Every perfect Ricochet has a hook within GALLOWS_REACH.

Water Table

Layer + rule. Any element. C1

Every later room has a two-pool channel along a wall, linked (links: ["pool"]). ice_rink on. Ice freezes the channel; storm makes it live.

Cold Iron Bond

Layer + rule. Storm or ice. C1

Every later room adds a row of three grates and links them (links: ["grate"]). One storm mark makes the row live; a Frost Spear on it raises a Frost Wall.

Deep Footings

Layer. Dark. C1

Every later room gets 2 more pillars in the open floor. Uproot by a pillar pays 244 against 71 in the open (cool_pass_check), so this is an Overwhelm build's Breach.

Rotten Floor

Layer. Anyone. C1

Every later room scatters 3 rubble and 2 spike tiles under the spawn points. Breaker landings on rubble throw chunks; thrown foes take x1.5 on spikes.

  • Replaces: about a third of the Boon pool. The state Boons and the 18 old cells stay. Gear and Techniques are untouched.
  • Reuses: ROOM_DEFS layers and the four room keys, env_flourish.on() (which already reads a room's flourishes list), env_piece.place() and its state, the cistern's aqueduct and the forge's powder line as ready-made layers, rooms_check's fit and overlap rules.
  • Cost: one C2 for the overlay (a run-wide dictionary that rooms.rule() and layout() merge over each room), then C1 per Breach: a placement list per layout, checked by rooms_check.
  • Nearest in other games: Into the Breach and Spelunky have rooms whose terrain is the puzzle. Isaac items that change every later floor ("Treasure Map", the curses) change the world for the rest of a run. The difference here: a Breach does not add a stat or a hazard. It adds things a named string already knows how to use, so the payoff is a combo you could already play in the forge, now in every room.
Risk: every room looks the same by floor 3. Three Breaches put kegs, hooks and pools in every room, and the layouts stop mattering. Hedges: the cap of 3, each layer placed by the layout (not everywhere), and Breaches never repeat. Early sign: Zak can no longer tell the cistern from the forge in floor 3.

2. Writs: take the warden's rule

Hook: each floor warden fights in a room built around one prison rule; beat it and that rule is yours for the rest of the run.

How it works in play

A Writ is a Breach-sized room key, forced and stronger. The warden's room has it on, and the warden is built so the rule is how you beat him. When he falls, the Writ goes into the run's overlay. That replaces run.html's warden reward (a Boon, 1 of 3). Only the two floor wardens give one; the final warden ends the run.

The Turnkey: Writ of Repair

Floor 1. Key: rebuild on. C1

His fight: run.html's warden from parts, an outsized skeleton warrior that a launcher does not lift. His room has four pillars and rebuild true, the training hall's rule: a toppled pillar comes back REBUILD_S 6 s later. Launches do nothing to him, so the damage is Overwhelm, then Uproot, again and again as the pillars return.

Taken: every later room rebuilds what you use up. Two kegs per keg spot, two Uproots per pillar.

The Sluicekeeper: Writ of the Flood

Floor 2. Key: links: ["pool"] + a channel layer. C3 (new warden, a Meshy body)

His fight: a Warded wader (blocks from every side until launched or grabbed) in a room cut by a long channel. The pools take every element: ice freezes the channel and a knocked escort slides the length of it (RINK_SLIDE_MIN); storm makes the water live; magma turns it to steam. Each is a way to strip his escorts while you work him open with a grab.

Taken: Water Table, but every pool in every later room is linked into one body.

Later wardens on the same pattern: the Hangman (Rusted Chain, the gallows; a foe that only drops guard when hung), the Pitchwarden (Powder Store; kegs ring his arena). A floor draws one of 2 wardens, so two runs meet different Writs. Each new warden is one Meshy foe (26 credits, no hand rigging, meshy-foes.md) plus a room.

Worked example

Ice run. F1 warden: the Turnkey. Frost Spear on his grate raises a Frost Wall, Harpoon's cubes Shatter on it, and every 6 s the pillars come back for another Uproot. He falls; Writ of Repair is taken. F2 R1 is the forge layout: the Frost Wall's grate rebuilds, so he can raise a second wall in the same fight. F2's warden is the Sluicekeeper: Harpoon freezes the channel and the cube row goes 24 m down it. Writ of the Flood taken. Floor 3: every pool in every room is one channel.

  • Replaces: the floor warden's Boon offer.
  • Reuses: rebuild (the Turnkey's Writ is a C0 key; his trait is the C1), the cistern's channel rules, the planned elite traits (Rooted, Warded), the Meshy foe route.
  • Cost: the Turnkey C1. Each new warden C3: a model, a trait, a room. No new effect sheets.
  • Nearest in other games: Mega Man: beat the boss, take its weapon. The difference: you take a rule of the room, not a gun, and the boss fight is the tutorial for the rule you take.
Risk: a Writ that the held spirit cannot use. A warden built on kegs is a bad fight for an ice heretic. Rule: a Writ's pieces must answer to the dark or to every element (pillars, hooks, pools, rebuild). Keg and grate wardens only appear when the held spirit answers them.

3. Bearers: elites wear the Gear

Hook: Gear does not come out of chests; an elite wears it, fights with it, and you take it off his body.

How it works in play

  • The trait is the item's family. run.html plans one trait per elite. Here the trait says what kind of item he wears. Rooted (a launcher does not lift it) bears a Graft from the force lines: Heavy Head, Long Reach, Hard Landing. Skittish (dodges like a rogue) bears a Shackle from the step lines: Quick Step, Long Step, Sky Grip. Warded (blocks from every side until launched or grabbed) bears a Shackle from the counter lines: Wide Watch, Iron Answer, Hurled.
  • You see it at the door. The elite door shows the item's floor card (loot.html's compare, unchanged). You choose the room by the item.
  • You see it on him. A Graft bearer has the glowing tentacle knot grown into his arm; a Shackle bearer wears the iron band. Rarity colour as on the floor.
  • He drops it where he dies. Then the floor card and the E swap, exactly as loot.html has it.

Worked example

F1 R4 doors: Rest, or an elite warrior bearing Prison Weight (Shackle, Hallowed: Wide Watch, Hurled). He is Warded, so the strings bounce off him; what opens him is the counter: Backlash on his windup, then Ricochet. Prison Weight is a counter item. By the time it drops he has spent a minute teaching it. F2: a brute bearing Hooked Barb (Long Reach), Rooted, so the answer is Overwhelm and the pin. The Graft that drops makes Reap, Black Tide and Overwhelm reach further.

  • Replaces: chest drops and the separate elite trait list. Elite room and Gear become one thing. Gear per run stays 2 to 3: one elite per floor, plus the rare foe drop.
  • Reuses: loot.html's items, lines and floor card; the elite traits run.html already plans; elites as their kind at x2 hp; the brute and the cultist as bearer bodies.
  • Cost: C2 per trait (3 of them, planned anyway). The band and knot on a foe are a separate mesh on hand.r (meshy-foes.md lists held meshes as not built): C2 for a tinted band, C4 for a real knot.
  • Nearest in other games: Monster Hunter (the gear is made from the thing you fought) and Shadow of Mordor captains. The difference: the item does not copy the elite's power; the trait points at the tool in your kit that beats it, and the item rewards that tool.
Risk: three traits are a thin item pool. If a trait always maps to the same three lines, bearers become predictable. That is also the point (you can read the door), so the hedge is Hallowed rolls two lines from the family, and Profane items come only from wardens.

4. The Haul: a deck of pieces, minted by kills

Hook: how a foe dies decides what piece it leaves; the arm hauls those pieces from room to room as a small deck, and E plants one mid-fight.

What a deckbuilder does, and what this does

Slay the Spire: you build a deck between fights, draw a hand each fight, and some cards exhaust (gone for that fight once played). Here the cards are pieces, the deck is minted by your kills, and playing one is not a turn: it is a press in a real-time fight. The card's effect is not written on the card. It is whatever your strings already do to that piece.

How it works in play

  • Minting. A kill that pays a rule leaves remains that match it. Burst kill: a pitch keg. Shatter kill: an ice slab (a short Frost Wall). Arc kill: a cold iron grate. Snap kill: a bone pillar. Crater kill: rubble. At the room's end you take 1 of the remains you made into the deck. Max deck 5.
  • Drawing. Each room deals 2 cards. They show as two small icons by the combo guide.
  • Planting. E (free since the old E move went; the bond prompt uses it only by a spirit) slams the top card into the floor where the tentacle points, snapped to a tile. It is a real piece with every verb.
  • Exhaust. A planted piece used up (a keg burst, a pillar uprooted, a slab shattered) leaves the deck for good. One never used comes back next room.

Worked example

Magma spirit. R1: a minion dies in a Burst; the deck takes a pitch keg. R2, hand: pitch keg. He plants it in the middle of the first wave, then L L L H: Molten Palm carries them into it, KA-BLOOOM, all molten. The keg is gone. R2 also: a warrior dies to a Snap in Overwhelm's tendrils; a bone pillar joins the deck. R3: the brute. He plants the pillar beside it, Overwhelm, Uproot: 244 by a pillar, 71 in the open.

  • Replaces: Gear, if it wins. Both are "the thing you carry and choose". Running both is two find layers.
  • Reuses: env_piece.place() and every verb, frost_wall.raise() for the slab, env_target.add_prop(), improv_paid for which rule killed, the brazier and pillar models.
  • Cost: C3. The plant press, placement checks mid-fight (a floor piece replaces its tile), the hand icons. C4 if the plant needs its own arm animation.
  • Nearest in other games: Slay the Spire for the deck and exhaust; Risk of Rain's Engineer and Dead Cells' traps for placing things in a real-time fight. The difference: the pieces are not turrets. They do nothing alone. They are set-ups the existing strings pay off.
Risk: a press that breaks the string. If E mid-fight costs the combo's rhythm, nobody plants. Test first in the training hall with debug cards: can Zak plant and go straight into L L L H in under a second? If not, plant only between waves.

5. The Augury: the floor as a tarot spread

Hook: at each floor's start you see its four rooms as four cards, and you may reverse one.

What tarot does, and what this does

A tarot spread lays cards in positions (past, present, future); a reversed card reads as the inverse. In Cult of the Lamb the tarot is a boon you draw. Here the cards are not powers. They are the rooms, read in advance, so you can pick Boons for rooms you know are coming.

How it works in play

  • Four cards, in order. Each is a room: its layout (The Drill Hall, The Cistern, The Forge, or one of run.html's 4 new layouts, such as a hook-lined Gallows) and its foe mix in one line. Shown in the training-hall door before floor 1 and at each warden's exit.
  • One reversal per floor. A reversed card flips its pieces' starting state. The Cistern reversed: The Frozen Cistern (pools start frozen). The Forge reversed: Damp Powder (kegs need two magma hits, and braziers start out). The Drill Hall reversed: Collapsing (pillars start cracked).
  • Doors stay. Doors still show the reward layer. The Augury only fixes which room is behind them.

Worked example

Floor 2 spread: Drill Hall, Forge, Cistern, Gallows. Ice spirit. The Forge's kegs are useless to him, so he reverses the Cistern to the Frozen Cistern. Now room 3 opens with a frozen channel, and he takes Brittle Crowd at room 2 knowing every Harpoon row in room 3 will slide 24 m and Shatter.

  • Replaces: run.html's random layout draw, and its "door shows the room's piece family, later" idea.
  • Reuses: ROOM_DEFS, piece state, the run seed.
  • Cost: C1 for the reversed states (data), C2 for the spread screen.
  • Nearest in other games: FTL and Slay the Spire show the route ahead. The difference: you do not pick a path. You read one fixed floor and bend one room of it.
Risk: a screen before the fight. run.html's rule is "if a choice needs a panel, cut it". The spread must read in five seconds: four room names, four piece icons, one reverse. If it needs more, cut it to "reverse the next room" on the door.

6. Patch Notes: your build as a move-list diff

Hook: every reward is shown as the lines it changes on your move list, written like a fighting game's patch notes.

What fighting games do, and what this does

Fighting-game players live in the move list, the training mode and the patch notes ("Dash Stab: recovery -3 frames"). Awaken already has a move list (move_list.gd), a combo guide that tags a row "+ Shatter" (hero_moves.pays()), and a bond prompt that lists what a spirit opens. Patch Notes uses that language for every reward, so the build reads as your version of the heretic.

How it works in play

  • The card body is the diff. "Dash Stab (Dash L): now leaves frozen, 1 s." "Ricochet: hangs the attacker on a hook within 10 m." "All rooms: + powder line."
  • The pause MOVES panel marks changed rows with the reward's name beside them.
  • The run ends with its notes: "Heretic, this run" and every line, in the order taken. Win or die.

Worked example: after F1, the notes read: "Molten Palm (L L L H): + Burst on molten foes (Wildfire spreads it). All rooms: + powder line (Powder Store). Every room rebuilds (Writ of Repair)." Three lines, one glance, the whole build.

  • Replaces: nothing. It is how loot.html section 6 (reading it in the fight) is written.
  • Reuses: move_list.gd rows, hero_moves.pays(), Spirits.offers()'s pattern of listing rows.
  • Cost: C0 to C1. Text and one mark on the move list.
  • Nearest in other games: real patch notes, and Balatro's plain joker text. The difference: it is your own patch, in the move list you train on.
Risk: a wall of text. Each line must name one row. A reward that cannot be written in one line on the move list fails loot.html's naming test anyway.

7. Specialists: the prison answers your build

Hook: the church keeps a ledger of how you kill, and floors 2 and 3 each add one foe sent to answer your favourite payoff.

How it works in play

  • The ledger is improv_paid, already counted per state. The most-paid rule on a floor picks the next floor's specialist.
  • A specialist answers its rule in an aura, and dies fast. A cultist body (26 hp), one per wave at most. Shatter build: the Thaw-priest thaws frozen foes within 4 m. Arc build: the Lightning Rod, where arcs end on him. Burst build: the Quencher, who puts out molten near him. Snap build: the Unbinder.
  • The answer is a target, not a wall. Kill him first and the build is back. Kill him with the rule he counters and he pays it double.

Worked example: floor 1 was all Shatter. Floor 2's second wave brings a Thaw-priest behind the shields. Harpoon's cubes thaw if they drift near him, so the room asks for Tow Line on him first, then the row.

  • Replaces: part of floors 2 and 3's foe mix (run.html's "flankers and a shooter screen").
  • Reuses: improv_paid, the cultist model, the state clear functions (_break_ice(), floor_bind.let_go_of()).
  • Cost: C2 per specialist (aura, tell), C3 with its own look.
  • Nearest in other games: Left 4 Dead's director and its special infected, each built to break one habit. The difference: here the special is chosen by your build, not your position.
Risk: it punishes commitment. A roguelike rewards going deep, and this pushes back. It is the one proposal here I would not build before the run is fun. If kept, the "pays double" rule must make the specialist feel like a gift to the build, not a tax.

Keep conventional

Zak: "we don't need to make EVERYTHING unique". These stay as loot.html and run.html have them, on purpose:

  • The Boon offer: 1 of 3, kept all run, no rerolls. Players know it instantly. The spin goes into one card in three (a Breach), not into how the offer works.
  • The state Boons and the 18 old cells. They are already unique, because the combat is. Shard Cut has its own word and look. Giving them a new frame adds nothing.
  • Gear's number lines and three rarities. "+15% reach" is fine. The find is the fun, and Bearers change where it is found, not what it says.
  • Two doors, no map, no currency. The Augury only fixes which room is behind a door.
  • Hazards stay hero-safe. Breaches and Writs make rooms richer for him, never deadlier. A room that hurts him is a difficulty layer, which is a different page.

This lane's pick

Breaches, with the Turnkey's Writ

Cheapest unique spin with the biggest visible change. Almost all of it is room data that exists. It finally gives the three flourishes and the two showcase rooms a job in a run. It changes the world, which no other lane covers. Build order: the overlay and three Breaches (Powder Store, Rusted Chain, Water Table) inside run.html's slice 4; the Writ of Repair with the Turnkey in slice 7.

Next, if it lands: The Haul

The real mechanical departure, and the deepest. Prototype it in the training hall with debug cards before any run uses it. Only one of the Haul and Gear should ship.

Free to do anytime: Patch Notes

Text only. Helps every layer read, including the other lanes' ideas.

Decisions for Zak

  1. Can one card in a Boon offer be a Breach, a room key that changes every later room? Yes, at most one per offer and 3 per run.
  2. Does a Breach bring the pieces it needs, so the gallows, ice rink and brazier spill leave Not yet? Yes. A Breach is never offered unless its pieces will be there and the held spirit or the dark answers them.
  3. Do floor wardens give their Writ instead of a Boon offer? Yes, starting with the Turnkey's Writ of Repair (rebuild on), which costs almost nothing.
  4. Prototype the Haul (E plants a piece minted by a kill)? Yes, but in the training hall only, after Boons are judged. It ships only if it replaces Gear.
  5. Do elites wear the Gear they drop (trait = item family)? Yes, when Gear is built (slice 5). It costs the traits run.html already plans.

Where the docs disagree with this lane's brief

  • One spirit or two Attunements. spirits.md: he holds one spirit at a time; claiming another swaps; a crossover (Fault Line, Hailstorm, Steam Burst) "needs both, so one spirit never opens it". The hub, kits.html and run.html assume two Attunements per run and three crossovers as Techniques. This page filters Breaches by the held spirit, which works either way, but the other pages' sample builds (Thunder Cage, storm and ice) do not exist under one spirit.
  • "Frost walls" are not a room piece. A Frost Wall is raised by Frost Spear on a grate (frost_wall.gd). The piece is the grate.
  • The world-changing items already have a draft. environment-lore.md's "How items could alter rooms" lists Sapper's Map, Cold Iron Bond, Water Table, Rusted Chain and Fallen Column. Breaches build on it. What is new here: they sit in the Boon offer, bring their own pieces, follow the spirit filter, and the wardens give them out.
  • The foe roster has grown. run.html's role table lists six skeleton kinds. enemy_types.gd also has the Meshy brute (120 hp, poise 14) and cultist (ranged, 26 hp). Both are used above.
