The spirit is the build

One of the unique-spin lanes. The question: instead of Boons and Gear sitting beside the spirit as separate menus, what if they run through it?

Make the spirit the face of the Boon layer. A Boon offer is three scraps of the prison floating out of a broken piece, and the spirit eats the one you pick. What it ate shows on it, and when a boon fires, the spirit flashes. Mechanically it is loot.html's Boon pool unchanged: same signs, same filter, 1 of 3, kept all run.

Its rank is how much it has eaten. Every 2 morsels it ranks up: rank II opens its element's big move, rank III its passive. The planned spirit rank-up and the Boon layer become one thing instead of two.

Then give it one job in a fight, and only one: when a breaker lands, it dives in after you and leaves its element's state on the pile. It never acts on its own.

The run's second element is a second spirit the arm swallows. It opens that element's ender and the crossovers, and stays out of sight, so the one you raise is still the one on your shoulder.

Keep Gear's drop-and-compare conventional. The find is the find.

Where the spirit stands today

  • Built (docs/features/spirits.md): three spirits in training, E opens the bond prompt, one held at a time, a swap sends the old one back. It opens its element's moves through Spirits.allows(). It floats at RIDE over his right shoulder and only follows.
  • Spirits.PASSIVES is three empty strings. No rank, no growth, no part in breakers.
  • One spirit never opens a crossover (Fault Line, Hailstorm, Steam Burst need two elements). The design hub's run holds two elements. So a second spirit, or something like it, is not optional.
  • The design pages (hub, kits, loot, run) predate the spirits and call taking an element an Attunement. On this page, bonding a spirit is the Attunement.

The fiction this page assumes

heretic.md says he broke into the vault and took all four powers, and the dark took his arm. But he starts a run with the dark alone. One small reading squares it: the church kept each power in a living keeper. When he broke the vault, the dark went into his arm and the other three keepers fled into the prison. The spirits are those keepers. He has to win each one back, and the prison was built to starve them, so they are small and hungry. Every grate, keg and chain in it is soaked in the power it was built against (environment-lore.md). That is what they eat.

This reading gives every proposal below a reason: the spirit eats the prison (1), watches the heretic (2), fights beside him only when he opens the way (3), and the dark that once held all four powers can hold a second one again (4).

1. Morsels: the spirit eats your boons

The Boon offer stays exactly as loot.html has it, but the cards are things the spirit eats, and what it eats shows on it. Cheap reskin.

  • How it plays. After a room, a broken piece (a burst keg, a toppled pillar, the room's chest) lets out three morsels: a shard of cistern ice, a forge rivet, a gallows hook link. Each is one boon from the pool, with its sign. Walk up, E, and the panel reads "Feed the STORM SPIRIT?" with the three boon lines. It eats the one you pick; the others fade. Signs, the "only what you can use" filter and "lean in" are unchanged.
  • It shows. Each sign it has eaten adds one small tell to the spirit, using the foe state marks from comic_state_tell.gd: a frost rim (frozen), a crackle (charged), an ember drip (molten), a violet tether (bound), a stone crust (buried), a wind curl (airborne). At most 3 tells show, the top signs. When a boon fires, the spirit flashes that tell. The bottom-left boon strip becomes the spirit itself.
  • Its rank is its meal count. Bond: the ender. After 2 morsels, rank II: the big move with its H ending. After 4, rank III: its passive (Spirits.PASSIVES). The planned rank-up needs no separate reward.
  • Worked example.
  1. Room 1Bond the storm spirit. L H is Updraft Lash. Rank I.
  2. Room 2Morsels: Overload, Torn Loose, Hang Time. Eat Overload. It starts to crackle.
  3. Room 3A Technique door: Galvanize.
  4. Room 4Eat Hang Time. Rank II: Live Wire (L H H) and Grounded open. The spirit grows a size.
  5. Room 8Fourth morsel. Rank III: storm's passive. Every Overload makes it flash green.
  • Replaces. The boon card screen and the boon strip. Folds the spirit's rank-up into the boon count. A side effect worth having: the bond in room 1 gives one new idea (the ender), and the big move arrives later, which fits the hub's "one new idea per room".
  • Reuses. The 18 cells in IMPROV_UPGRADE through Kit.set_on(); bond_prompt.gd and Spirits.offers() (the panel already lists what you get); the state marks as icons; the spirit models.
  • Cost. The boons themselves as loot.html (8 cells at C0). The feed prompt C1 (a bond_prompt sibling). The six tells and the flash C2 (small designed sprites riding the spirit). A bigger model per rank C4, later.
  • Risk. The spirit is SIZE 0.8 m, behind his shoulder, seen from above at 90 px. The tells may not read at all. If so, the flash on fire is the only tell that matters, and that is still more than a strip. Second: tying rank to boons means a Rest room's heal (which replaces a boon in run.html) also costs rank. That may be fine (a real choice) or annoying.
  • Nearest thing. Hades: boons come from a character, so picking one feels like a relationship. Different here: the giver is not a god you visit but the creature you carry, the boons pile up on it where you can see them, and its growth is the boon count.

2. Lessons: it learns what you do

The spirit watches which payoffs you land, and the next offer comes from what it saw. Mechanical, small.

  • How it plays. The spirit keeps a tally per sign for the room: every rule it saw paid (Shatter, Arc, Burst, Snap, Juggle, Crater, from _pay()), every breaker landing, every Backlash, every dash through a foe, every Awakening. The next offer draws two cards from your top two signs and one wild. This replaces loot.html's "lean in" rule (one card from a sign you hold) with "lean into what you did".
  • Mastery cards. A sign's tally over the whole run past 25 unlocks its one mastery card (a rank II number boon, or a rule bend like Wildfire or Deep Grave). You can only be offered it by doing it.
  • It says so. The offer has one line under the cards: "It watched 14 Shatters." That line is the whole UI.
  • Worked example. Ice spirit. Rooms 1 and 2: you Harpoon (Dash L H) and Tow Line through the cubes, so frozen leads. Offer: Shard Cut, Brittle Crowd, and a wild Cold Step. Rooms 5 and 6, against shield foes, you start countering; the next offer holds Riposte Chain and Static Parry. By room 9 the frozen tally passes 25 and the offer holds frozen's mastery card, Long Thaw II. The build followed the fights you had.
  • Replaces. The "lean in" rule. Everything else in the Boon offer stays.
  • Reuses. _pay(), player.breakers_landed, counters, counter_perfects: the autotest already counts all of these ([improv], [counter]).
  • Cost. C2: one tally dictionary, the offer's weighting, one text line.
  • Risk. A loop: you only see what you already do, and builds narrow early. The wild card is the brake; if runs still feel the same, make it two wild cards.
  • Nearest thing. Learning by doing (Final Fantasy II, Skyrim) and Hades keepsakes that level with use. Different here: use does not raise a number, it steers which rule bends you are offered.

3. The second blow: the spirit strikes on your beats

The spirit never fights on its own. It joins exactly two moments the player earned: a breaker landing and a perfect counter. Real departure.

  • How it plays. When a breaker lands (breaker_impact()), the spirit drops off his shoulder onto the zone 0.25 s later, during his float down, and leaves its element's state on everyone the slam hit: storm charged, ice frozen, magma buried. On a perfect Backlash it strikes the attacker the same way. Then it flies back to RIDE. It has no hp, no AI, no target of its own, and nothing can hit it.
  • Why only there. The breaker window and the perfect counter are the game's two timing tests. The spirit's blow is a reward for passing one, not a turret. It turns every breaker into the start of the next payoff, which is loot.html's Aftermark boon, given a body.
  • Tricks. Some morsels (proposal 1) teach the blow a trick instead of bending a rule: Linger (it hangs over the zone 2 s and puts its state on anyone who walks in), Fetch (it drags the foe it struck 2 m toward him), Echo (on a PERFECT it strikes twice). One trick at a time.
  • Worked example.
  1. Room 2Storm. Live Wire into Grounded. The spirit dives and charges the pile. Reap next pays Arc through all of them.
  2. Room 4Eat the Linger trick. A PERFECT Grounded leaves it hovering; the shield foe walks in and comes out charged.
  3. Room 7A perfect Backlash on the warrior: the spirit charges it before Ricochet throws it into the crowd. Short Circuit cashes the lot.
  • Replaces. The Aftermark boon (it is now what the spirit does, from rank I or II). Part of the boon pool becomes tricks.
  • Reuses. The spirit_companion node (a tween to the zone and back), the existing state setters (freeze, charge, the buried of Rockslide), spirit_echo.strike() for its hit, the designed sheets for its landing.
  • Cost. C3: a new visible behaviour with existing sheets. Each trick C2.
  • Risk. The breaker landing is already the loudest frame in the game (crater, war cry, name callout). A dive on top may be noise. It must land after the callout, small, and in the float down. Second: a free state on every landing is strong; the "one state per landing" rule and the per-foe wait from loot.html's stacking rules apply.
  • Nearest thing. Devil May Cry 5's V (familiars do the fighting) and Nier Automata's pod (fires all the time). Both are on the other side: the companion carries the damage. Here it carries none of it, and only follows up a beat you landed.

4. The swallowed spirit: the second element goes into the arm

The run's second element is a second spirit, and it does not ride. The arm eats it. Structural departure, little combat change.

  • How it plays. Floor 2 room 1 offers the other two spirits on two doors. Bond one: the tentacle wraps it and swallows it (the dark held all four powers once). It opens its element's ender and every crossover of the pair. It does not eat, rank, or strike. Its colour shows on the tentacle tips only.
  • The relation. The rider is the one you raise. The swallowed one is a tool. At a Rest room you may swap them instead of healing: the swallowed comes out at rank I with nothing eaten, and the old rider goes into the arm and keeps the boons it ate (they are in you now), but stops growing. A swap is a second growth track for the last floor, paid for with a heal.
  • Worked example. Floor 1 with the storm rider, rank III. Floor 2 room 1: take ice. The arm swallows it, the tips go pale cyan, L L H is Frost Spear, and the next Technique offer holds Hailstorm. Floor 3 Rest room: swap. Ice rides from rank I, storm sits in the arm with Overload and Hang Time still on, and the last floor's morsels build an ice side.
  • Replaces. The second Attunement. Gives the crossovers a home the one-spirit rule lacks.
  • Reuses. Spirits.allows() (it already checks a crossover's pair; it needs "held" to be two), the bond prompt, the element tints on the arm.
  • Cost. C2: two held, the swap at Rest, checks for every pair. The tip tint C1. A visible bulge of the swallowed spirit in the arm C4, later.
  • Risk. The swap may never be worth a heal, so nobody uses it; fine, it costs little. Bigger: players lose track of which elements are open with only a tint to say so. The combo guide already shows only what routes, which carries most of it.
  • Nearest thing. Kirby's swallow-to-copy, and a Pokémon lead with one in reserve. Different here: the second element is deliberately the lesser half, so there is always one creature to raise, and one thing on screen.

5. Vessels: the spirit wears the loot

The Shackle slot becomes the spirit's vessel, a prison object it climbs into, so loot shows on the spirit and not on the heretic. Cheap reskin of one Gear slot.

  • Why. heretic.md bans ornaments: no buckles, no jewellery, no pouches. loot.html had to squeeze two slots out of the arm and the shackle. The spirit is a free canvas: anything can hang on it without touching his silhouette.
  • How it plays. The second-slot drops are vessels: a Warden's Lantern, a Cracked Bell, a Censer, a Gaol Key. The spirit climbs in and floats as that object, in its element's colour, its own eyes showing through. Lines are the Shackle pool unchanged (dash, counter, Awakening); with proposal 3 they can also carry tricks. Profane vessels carry the rites (Cuff of the Unshriven becomes the Unshriven Censer). The floor card, the E swap, the rarities and "lost at run end" are loot.html's.
  • Worked example. Room 5's chest: a Cracked Bell (Hallowed: Wide Watch, Kindling). The storm spirit climbs in; a green-crackling bell rides his shoulder. Room 9 elite: the Gaol Key (Profane, Gallows Chain's rite). Bell on the floor, key over the shoulder.
  • Replaces. The Shackle's look and fiction. Keeps its lines. The Graft stays on the arm.
  • Reuses. loot.html's Shackle pool, floor card and swap; the spirit's model slot; the meshy.py spirit pipeline (spirits are unrigged models, the cheap kind).
  • Cost. Lines C1 as before. A tinted generic vessel C2. A real model per vessel C4 each.
  • Risk. A spirit inside a lantern stops looking like a creature you raise; keep it visible round the object. And it is a fourth thing to read on him (hero, arm, spirit, vessel).
  • Nearest thing. Destiny's Ghost shells, almost exactly. Different here: a shell carries real gear lines, drops in the run, and is lost at its end.

6. Temperament: what it remembers

At a run's end the spirit keeps one thing: the sign it ate most, as a nudge for next time. Small mechanical, meta.

  • How it plays. Each of the three spirits has one saved field. When a run ends (death or win), its rider's top sign becomes its temperament. Next time you bond it, the prompt says "It remembers: frozen", and its first offer is sure to hold one card of that sign. No free boon, no power, only a nudge. At the end of a run you can instead release it, clearing the field.
  • Worked example. Run 1: the ice spirit ate three frozen boons and you died on floor 2. Run 2: bond ice; the prompt says "It remembers: frozen"; room 2's offer holds Shard Cut. Run 3 you want a counter build, so you released it after run 2; room 2's offer is fully open.
  • Replaces. Nothing in the run. It is a candidate for run.html's "what carries between runs".
  • Reuses. The user:// records the game already saves; the offer filter.
  • Cost. C1.
  • Risk. Any carry-over invites a stronger one next. Keep it a nudge forever. run.html owns this call.
  • Nearest thing. Pokémon natures, Rogue Legacy traits. Different here: it is the same creature run to run, and its trait is a record of how you played it.

Keep conventional

  • The Graft and the find. A drop, a floor card, keep or leave. It is the one moment a run gives you something you did not choose. Players already know how to read it, and it needs no teaching.
  • The numbers. Affix lines stay plain ("dash cooldown 0.25 s"). Dressing a number in fiction makes it harder to compare, not more fun.
  • Techniques. A new press is already the most distinctive reward the game has. It does not need a spirit wrapper.
  • The spirit never gets hp, AI, its own attacks or a mood that changes in a fight. Each of those is a feel pass that competes with the heretic's combat, which is the game's strength. A mood that shifts in real time is the first thing I would cut from any version of this.

This lane's pick

Morsels now, the second blow after

Ship proposal 1 inside run slice 4 (Boons): it costs little over the plain card screen and turns the spirit's rank-up and the Boon layer into one thing. Take proposal 4 when floor 2 is built: the second element needs a home anyway. Try proposal 3 only once Boons are judged; if picking a boon still feels like picking a number, the blow is the fix.

Everything through the spirit

All six. The most distinctive version, and the one that repeats the old run's mistake: wide before the combat is proven against a crowd. Not now.

Reskin only

Proposals 1 and 5. Almost free and the spirit becomes the run's face, but it still only follows. Fine as a floor if 3 fails its feel pass.

Decisions for Zak

  1. Is a Boon something the spirit eats, with its rank counted in meals? Yes. Same pool and rules as loot.html; rank II at 2, rank III at 4.
  2. Does the bond give only the ender, with the big move at rank II? Yes. One new idea in room 1, and the rank-up means something.
  3. Does the spirit ever fight? Only on a breaker landing and a perfect counter, and only after Boons are judged in play.
  4. Is the run's second element a second spirit swallowed into the arm? Yes. It opens the ender and the crossovers, and only the rider grows.
  5. Do the spirits remember anything between runs? Not yet. If run.html wants a carry-over, temperament (a nudge, never power) is the one to try.
