Earned in the fight

One of the four "unique spin" lanes. This one asks: what if the build is shaped by how you fight, not chosen from a card screen between fights?

Best single idea: The Window Learns. A breaker's second ending is not a reward card. You learn it in the fight. Land the first ending while the room is in the situation the second ending is built for, and the game stamps the second ending as learned. From then on, that window shows both chips.

Example: Undertow's Meteor Fall lands while Molten Palm has scattered the foes. Meteor Shower is built for scattered foes, so it is learned on that landing. No menu, no grind. It teaches the "L wins when..., H wins when..." line that every window already has.

The endings are already built and each one already has a tested best situation. The work is a gate, a check at the landing and a stamp: about C2 once, then C1 per ending.

Cheap one to ship with Boons: The Last Word. How the room's last foe dies picks the sign of one card in the next Boon offer, and a peak style rank of S adds a fourth card. Nothing ever takes a card away.

Keep the Boon card screen, the Gear compare card and the numbers on Gear conventional. They are quick to read. The combat is where the novelty belongs.

The idea, and the trap

The spirit already feels like Awaken's own: you walk up to it, it rides on your shoulder, and it opens moves. Boons and Gear are what other games have. This lane lets the combat systems we already have decide what you are offered: named strings, timed breakers with a PERFECT, two-ending windows, the six foe states, the dash counter and the style rank. Sometimes they also decide what the reward is.

The trap is a system that pays only players who are already good, or that makes people farm one move on fodder instead of fighting well. Every proposal below passes three rules:

  • Situations, not counts. Nothing is earned by doing the same thing N times. Things are earned by doing something once in the right place.
  • Skill adds, never subtracts. A weak room gives what the current plan gives. A strong room gives a bit more choice, not more power.
  • Never stop the fight. No card or menu opens while foes are alive. Anything taken mid-fight is taken with a verb he already has.

Words used here: "spirit" is the built thing (spirits.gd). The design pages call the same reward an "Attunement". The cost classes C0 to C4 are loot.html's.

1. The Last Word (cheap: one rule on the Boon offer)

The way the room's last foe dies decides the sign of one card in the next Boon offer.

How it works in play

  • The last foe's killing blow is tagged with a cause. Every cause maps onto one of loot.html's ten signs, so no new grouping is needed:
    Last foe dies to...Sign of one card
    a rule payoff: Shatter, Arc, Burst, Snap, Juggle, Crater (or its upgrade cell)that state
    a breaker landing (State.LEAP)breaker
    Backlash or Ricochetcounter
    Dash Stab, Tow Line, an air-dash cutdash
    any hit while AwakenedAwakening
    anything else (a plain light, a stray hit)no steer: the plain offer
  • The room's peak style rank, not its final rank, sets the card count. S or higher: 4 cards. Below S: 3, which is what the plan already gives. Style already rewards variety over mashing (Game.register_hit: a new move is worth 3, a repeat 1.5), so the rank asks for mixing, not for speed.
  • The look: the last-kill slow motion already plays. On top of it, the sign's mark (the one comic_state_tell.gd draws on foes) stamps onto the body in ink. When the offer opens, the steered card carries the same stamp.
  • loot.html's "only what you can use" filter still applies. If the steered sign has nothing your kit can trigger, the card falls back to a plain one.

Worked example

RoomWhat happenedOffer
F1 R2Storm spirit. Galvanize charged the pack; the last skeleton dies when Reap arcs through it. Peak rank A.3 cards, one from the charged sign (Conduct). He takes it.
F1 R3He ends the room in Grounded's hang. Peak rank S.4 cards, one from the breaker sign (Aftermark).
F1 R4A rough room: hit often, peak rank B, the last foe dies to Triple Chop.3 plain cards, the same as the current plan. Nothing lost.
  • A struggling player: sees exactly today's plan. Steering comes from where they choose to finish, not from execution. Ending a room with a counter is hard; ending it on a frozen foe is easy.
  • Replaces: nothing. It is a rule on loot.html's offer. It also steers better than the door icon alone, because it shows up where the player's attention already is.
  • Reuses: the ten signs, the state tells, the last-kill slow motion, Game.rank(), the rule names from _pay().
  • Cost: C1. A kill-cause tag on the foe's death, one offer rule and the stamp, using an existing tell. It needs the Boon offer screen from run slice 4, which is planned anyway.
Risk: nobody notices the steer. If the stamp reads as decoration, the rule is invisible. Test: after five rooms, can Zak say why a card was offered? If not, add one line on the card: "Your last word: frozen."

Nearest thing elsewhere. Hades shows which god's boon is behind a door, and Devil May Cry grades a mission and pays orbs for the grade. The difference here: you steer in the middle of the fight by how you finish it, and the grade only ever adds a choice. It never adds power.

2. The Window Learns (the pick)

A breaker's second ending is learned by landing the first ending in the situation the second ending is built for.

How it works in play

Every built two-ending window has a best situation for each ending. void_arm_check._breaker_pairs() asserts it: each ending beats its sibling in its own situation and loses in the other one. At the start of a run, each window shows only its RMB ending. When you land that ending while the room is in the LMB ending's situation, the game stamps the LMB ending as learned. From then on, the prompt shows both chips.

Window (RMB ending)Learn the LMB ending...by landing RMB whenExisting check
Undertow (Meteor Fall)Meteor Showerthe palm scattered them: no group of 3 within METEOR_PILE_Rmeteor_target()

  • Any landing counts. No PERFECT is needed. A miss or a lockout teaches nothing, but costs nothing either.
  • The stamp: on that landing, after the callout banner, the second chip draws in ink under the circle with "LEARNED". The HUD's "New combo: ... 5 / 16 found" hint gets a sibling: "New ending: Meteor Shower (L in Undertow's window)".
  • The guide teaches it. While the LMB ending is unlearned and its situation is live, the prompt draws a dim ghost chip. The player sees "this could have been something else here" before it is learned.
  • Cold Front stays a card. It has no situation of its own (combo-breakers.md, cool pass round 52). It remains an ordinary Technique.
  • Learned for the run only. The next run starts with RMB endings again. That is fine, because every run meets walls, crowds and scatters.

Worked example

RoomWhat happenedKit change
F1 R1Bonds the ice spirit. Harpoon gathers a row and he lands Shatterstorm next to the room's east wall.Slapshot learned. Harpoon's window now offers punching the row into the wall.

  • A struggling player: learns endings from where foes happen to stand, not from execution. They also get the clearest lesson the game can give: this ending is for this situation. A player who never meets a crowd never gets Tempest, and does not need it.
  • Replaces: the "new ending in a breaker window" kind of Technique card. Those card slots go back to moves and crossovers. A Technique offer shrinks to "a new input".
  • Reuses: seven built and judged endings, their situation tests, the prompt's chips (hero_leap.options() just filters out the unlearned alt), the found-combo hint, and the Kit.has() gate pattern.
  • Cost: C2 once (the gate in options(), a situation check at _impact(), the ghost chip, the stamp), then C1 per ending (one condition each, mostly a call to an existing function). No new sheets. The checks need a bot string per ending that sets its situation first. The breaker-pairs layouts already do this (spread, wall).
Risk: a button that did nothing. Before the ending is learned, L in that window is dead. A player who presses L by habit gets nothing. Fix: before learning, L dives as RMB does. Test it in prompt_follow_check with the alt gated.
Risk: the situation is ambiguous. "A wall in reach" is clear. "Scattered" is fuzzier. Use the same pure functions the breakers already aim with, so the game and the stamp never disagree.

Nearest thing elsewhere. Final Fantasy IX teaches abilities through use, and fighting games have combo trials. The difference here: you learn by meeting a situation once, not by repeating something, and what you learn is the answer to the situation you were just in.

3. Trophy Grafts (a reskin of Gear drops)

The arm eats what it kills. An elite's drop is shaped by the move that killed it.

How it works in play

  • Fiction: "the arm is the character" (heretic.md). When an elite dies, the tentacle tears off a piece of it, and that piece is the Graft or Shackle.
  • The killing blow picks one affix line from loot.html's pools. The move that killed it decides the line:
    Killing moveLine
    Slice, Hurl, Triple ChopQuick Form (Graft)
    Reap, Black Tide, OverwhelmLong Reach (Graft)
    Harpoon, ShatterstormMany Tips (Graft)
    any breaker landingHard Landing (Graft) or Kindling (Shackle)
    Backlash, RicochetIron Answer or Hurled (Shackle)
    Dash Stab, Tow LineLong Step or Tow Weight (Shackle)
    The rest of the piece follows loot.html: the elite's one-rarity-up, and a random second line on a Hallowed.
  • A PERFECT on the killing breaker or counter gives a choice, not power. Two pieces fall, both carrying the killing line. You keep one.
  • Taken with the tentacle. If the slot is empty, the piece flies into the arm. If not, it lies on the floor as loot.html describes, and the grab (H from neutral, near the piece) swaps it as E does. The compare card stays shut while foes are alive.
  • Chest drops stay random. Only elites are trophies (wardens give Boons, run.html).

Worked example

RoomKillDrop
F1 R4 elite (shield)PERFECT counter, Ricochet into the hookTwo Hallowed Shackles, both with Hurled. He keeps the one that also has Wide Watch (Prison Weight).
F2 R4 elite (warrior)Shatterstorm on a row of cubesHallowed Graft with Many Tips (Censer Chain).
F3 R2 elite, a player who mashesTriple ChopHallowed Graft with Quick Form: it makes the thing they actually do faster.
  • A struggling player: always gets the drop, and it improves the move they used. PERFECT only adds a second option.
  • Replaces: the random affix roll on elite drops. The slots, rarities and compare card stay.
  • Reuses: loot.html's 16 lines, the chest piece, the grab (GR), the counter's counter_perfects, breaker_perfects.
  • Cost: C1 (the kill-to-line table) plus C2 for pickup by the grab, which needs a feel check so the grab never grabs loot when a foe is in reach.
Risk: stray kills. If a burn tick or an Arc link kills the elite, the "killing move" is noise. Rule: credit the last named combo that hit it within 1.5 s.

Nearest thing elsewhere. Mega Man takes each boss's weapon, and Devil May Cry's devil arms come from bosses. The difference here: how you killed it decides the piece, not whom you killed.

4. The Combo Book (a real departure for Boons)

Some boons are ink. Ink binds to the next named string you land, and it changes that string for the run.

How it works in play

  • Fiction: the heretic broke into the church's vaults. The move list is his stolen book of rites, and a gloss is what he writes in its margin.
  • Some cards in a Boon offer are glosses. Each names a payload, not a target:
    • Cold Ink: the string's last hit leaves frozen 1 s.
    • Spark Ink: the string's last hit leaves charged.
    • Grave Ink: the string's last hit leaves buried.
    • Bell Ink: the string's breaker window closes 0.08 s later.
    • Step Ink: the string's last hit resets the dash.
  • Once taken, the gloss is a blot of ink on the arm. The next named combo you land takes it. It is then written on that string's row in the pause MOVES panel and tagged on its combo guide row. That is the build screen: the move list you already have.
  • For players who want to choose: the pause book lets you pick the string by hand before the blot is used. Once written, it stays for the run.
  • One gloss per string. A string can be both a gloss's host and a Last Word cause.
  • The 18 element cells stay ordinary Boons. They are already per state and per element, so they need no host.

Worked example

RoomGlossLanded onWhat changes
F1 R2Cold InkReap (H L)The crescent leaves the crowd frozen. Singularity right after pays Shatter on all of them.
F1 R3Step InkUpdraft Lash (L H)Launch, dash at once, Dash Stab the next foe: a new route that is not on the card.
F2 R2, a player who mostly plays the light stringSpark InkTriple ChopThe plain string now sets up Arc. The ink went where they already play.
  • A struggling player: the ink lands on the strings they use most, so it strengthens what they can already do. Nobody is asked to land a string they cannot.
  • Replaces: loot.html's new rule-bend Boons that only make sense on a few moves (Cold Step, Slow Bell in part). Also the Profane rites' role of changing one move. A gloss is a player-placed rite, so Profane could drop to one per slot or go away.
  • Reuses: the state setters (freeze, charge, bury), the dash reset from Free Fall's design, the breaker _WINDOW keys, the guide's tag slot (the one "+ Shatter" uses), the MOVES panel rows.
  • Cost: C3. One generic hook on a named combo's last connected hit and on its window is C2. But every payload on every string is a combination someone must play: 5 glosses on about 16 strings is 80 pairs.
Risk: the combinations. Some pairs will break a route or a payoff (Cold Ink on Frost Spear is pointless, since ice on ice refreezes; Step Ink on a breaker's ender may cancel its leap). Fix: each gloss lists the hosts it refuses, and the blot skips them. Start with two glosses and judge them first.
Risk: binding by accident. A blot meant for Live Wire lands on Hurl. The hand-pick in the pause book is the safety valve. If Zak feels it is needed every time, make the hand-pick the default and keep the auto-bind as an option.

Nearest thing elsewhere. Path of Exile's support gems are linked to one skill. Diablo III has runes on skills, and Hades' Daedalus Hammer changes one attack. The difference here: you link the gloss by landing the string in a fight, and the result lives on the combo guide row.

5. Spirit Vows (risk as currency, without punishing)

At the door, the spirit asks for a deed in the next room. Do it, and the spirit ranks up early.

How it works in play

  • The planned spirit rank-up (more moves, then a passive) happens at a fixed pace, say at each warden. A vow is a way to get there sooner.
  • At each door the spirit offers one vow from three, or none: one easy, one middle, one hard. The vow is always something you do, never something you avoid:
    • Easy: "Land 3 different named combos."
    • Middle: "End 2 foes with a breaker."
    • Hard: "Counter a strike, then land a breaker on that same foe."
    • Element flavoured: storm "Arc through 4 foes at once", ice "Shatter a cube into a wall", magma "Burst a foe beside a burning keg".
  • Kept: the spirit ranks up at the room's end, not at the warden. A rank only arrives earlier. It is never bigger.
  • Broken or not taken: nothing is lost. The spirit ranks up on its normal schedule.
  • The vow sits as one line under the style rank and ticks as you progress. The spirit flares its element's colour when the vow is kept.

Worked example

RoomVowResult
F1 R2Middle: end 2 foes with a breakerKept. Storm spirit rank 2 now: Live Wire's air route opens, two rooms early.
F1 R3Hard: counter, then a breaker on the same foeBroken (the Ricochet killed it). Nothing lost.
F1 R4None takenThe spirit reaches rank 3 at the warden as normal.
  • A struggling player: can ignore vows and lose nothing. The easy vow asks for variety, the same thing style rank asks for.
  • Replaces: nothing in Boons or Gear. It gives the planned spirit rank-up a way to be earned.
  • Reuses: counters the game already keeps (combos_landed, breakers_landed, counters, endings_seen, the rule payoffs' [improv] counts), the spirit companion, the door.
  • Cost: C2 for the vow tracker and the line under the rank. It assumes the spirit rank-up exists, which is not built.
Risk: players feel they must take the vow. If a vow is always worth it, it becomes homework. Keep the reward small (earlier, never bigger) and keep the room's main reward separate.

Nearest thing elsewhere. Hades' Chaos boons and its Pact of Punishment, and the challenge missions in Devil May Cry and Bayonetta. The difference here: a vow is a deed, never a handicap. Breaking it costs nothing, and what it pays is time, not power.

Considered and left out

Moves that grow by use count ("land Reap 20 times, it grows"). This is the grind trap: it pays spamming one move on fodder, which works against style rank's variety rule. The Window Learns is the situation-based form of the same wish.
Style rank sets rarity. The rich get richer: a struggling player gets weaker gear and falls further behind. Rank adds one card in the Last Word, and that is all.
No-hit rewards. They teach players to play scared, and the dash counter is already the game's defensive verb. Reward counters, never avoidance.
Choosing a reward inside a breaker's hang (L takes one card, R the other). The hang is about 0.6 s real time. Nobody can read two cards in that time, and the hang belongs to the breaker.

Keep conventional

  • The 1-of-3 Boon card. It is fast, read once and understood by everyone. The Last Word changes what is on it, not how it looks.
  • The 18 element cells as plain Boons. They are already finished and per state. Making them special adds nothing.
  • Gear's two slots, three rarities and compare card. The loot moment works because it is familiar. Trophy Grafts changes only which line you get.
  • Fixed numbers on Gear lines. A plain "+15% reach" is a relief next to the strings.
  • Doors, Rest and the heal. Run structure is not where this game is different.

The reason: every new behaviour costs a feel pass, and Zak's feel passes are the bottleneck. Spend them on what touches the combat, and let everything around it read instantly.

The pick, and decisions for Zak

Pick: The Window Learns

It is the most "this game" of the five. It turns the combat's own design rule (every combo has a best situation) into the build, and it reuses seven finished endings. It also answers your round 43 note: "we can later incentivize people to do one or the other". Ship The Last Word next to it as the cheap Boon spin.

Runner-up: The Combo Book

It is the biggest departure and the most distinctive Boon layer. But it is C3 with a combination count that needs real play. Try two glosses after Boons are judged.

  1. Are second endings learned in the window (by landing the first ending in the second ending's best situation) instead of offered as Technique cards? Yes, for the seven with a situation. Cold Front stays a card.
  2. Before an ending is learned, does L in its window do the RMB ending? Yes. A dead button reads as a bug.
  3. Does the room's last kill steer one card's sign in the next Boon offer? Yes, using the ten signs as they are.
  4. Can style rank ever shrink an offer or lower a rarity? No. A peak of S adds a fourth card. Nothing takes one away.
  5. Does an elite's killing move pick its drop's affix line (Trophy Grafts)? Yes, for elite drops. Chests stay random.
